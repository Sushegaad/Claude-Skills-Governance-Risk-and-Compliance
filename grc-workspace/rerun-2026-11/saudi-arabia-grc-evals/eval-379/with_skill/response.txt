Short answer: not in final, binding form as of November 2026. The NCA has a draft, but you should not treat it as an enforceable requirement. AI systems are still caught by existing binding rules.

1. What exists on AI cybersecurity
- The NCA's draft AI Cybersecurity Guidelines (AICG-1:2026) went through public consultation from 5 July to 5 August 2026. The draft has 4 domains and about 42 guidelines and covers generative and agentic AI.
- As of November 2026 the final version is unpublished. Do not cite it as binding. Check nca.gov.sa for publication, and for whether the final text is a guideline or mandatory.

2. What binds you today when you use or build AI

| Instrument | Regulator | Relevance to AI |
|---|---|---|
| Saudi PDPL (M/19, amended by M/148) and its Regulations | SDAIA | Binding if AI processes personal data of Saudi residents. You need a lawful basis, notices, purpose limitation and minimisation, safeguards, a transfer mechanism for offshore models or APIs, and 72-hour breach notification to SDAIA. |
| NCA ECC-2:2024 | NCA | Mandatory if you are a government entity or subsidiary or a CNI operator. AI systems sit within its asset, access, data, and third-party and cloud controls. |
| NCA NCNICC-1:2025 | NCA | Mandatory and size-based for private non-CNI entities (Category A or B). AI tools fall under its cybersecurity governance, defence, and third-party and cloud components. |
| NCA CCC and CST Cloud Framework | NCA / CST | Apply if the AI is cloud-hosted for ECC-covered entities. Government data must stay in the Kingdom. Level 3 and 4 customer data under the CST framework requires in-Kingdom hosting. |
| Sector rules | SAMA / CMA | For example SAMA CSF for regulated financial entities, which stacks on the above. |

I am not covering SDAIA's AI ethics or other AI governance guidance in detail here. If you need that, tell me, and verify with SDAIA.

3. How to prepare
- Treat the AICG draft as a design signal, not a compliance obligation.
- Inventory AI use, including shadow use of generative tools and agents, and record the data each tool touches.
- Apply the existing controls to AI: access control, logging, vendor risk review, data classification limits on what may be sent to models, and residency for government and Level 3 and 4 data.
- Run a PDPL assessment for any AI use involving personal data, and check transfers for offshore AI services.
- Set up a watch on NCA and SDAIA publications, and plan a gap assessment once the AICG is final.
