Short answer: a 40-person private SME supplier is generally not directly bound by the NCA controls, but it can become bound contractually through you. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

1. Direct applicability
- NCA's ECC applies mandatorily to government bodies and private organizations operating, owning or hosting critical national infrastructure. An ordinary SME supplier outside those groups has no direct statutory ECC obligation.
- NCA has published lighter-weight material for smaller organizations (a cybersecurity guide for SMEs). It is voluntary and aimed at the basics.

2. Indirect applicability through your contract
- If you are subject to ECC or have to meet customer-imposed controls, ECC's third-party controls expect you to include cybersecurity requirements in supplier contracts, assess supplier risk, and make sure suppliers with access to your data or systems meet equivalent standards.
- If the supplier touches government or CNI data or systems, the flow-down is stronger and may include the Third-Party Cybersecurity Controls and, for cloud or hosted services, the Cloud Cybersecurity Controls (CCC).
- As you are a non-CNI retailer, you can set the level yourself. A proportionate approach is best.

3. Proportionate baseline to request from a 40-person supplier
- Named cybersecurity owner and a short approved security policy.
- Asset inventory and access control, with MFA on email, remote access and admin accounts.
- Endpoint protection, patching, secure configuration, email filtering and basic network security.
- Encrypted, tested backups.
- Incident reporting to you within an agreed time (for example 24 to 72 hours).
- Staff awareness training.
- Data protection and PDPL-aligned handling where they process your customers' personal data, with a processor agreement and breach notification terms.
- Right to request evidence (questionnaire, attestation or certificate), and audit rights proportionate to risk.
- Subcontractor flow-down and secure return or deletion of data on termination.

4. How to apply it
- Tier suppliers by risk: access to personal or sensitive data, network connectivity, criticality to operations. Only high-risk suppliers need detailed ECC mapping or an independent assessment.
- Offer a simplified control checklist, a realistic timeline for improvement, and accept an improvement plan as an alternative to a full pass for low-risk suppliers.
- Note that the PDPL applies to the supplier directly as a processor if it handles personal data, regardless of size.

5. Caveats
- Verify the current NCA documents and any contractual obligations you inherit. I cannot confirm from memory any 2025 to 2026 changes to the SME guidance.
