Short answer: if you are a private-sector retailer that is not designated critical national infrastructure (CNI), the NCA's Essential Cybersecurity Controls (ECC) are not automatically legally binding on you. In practice, though, several routes can make NCA controls apply, so you should check each one. This is general guidance, not legal advice. Confirm current versions and your status with NCA and counsel.

1. Mandatory scope of the ECC
- ECC (ECC-1:2018, superseded by ECC-2:2024) is mandatory for government entities (ministries, authorities, establishments) and for private-sector organizations that own, operate or host CNI. Other private-sector organizations are "encouraged" to adopt it.
- Confirm you have not been designated CNI or notified by NCA or a sector regulator. Retail is not normally a CNI sector, but a payments, logistics, telecom or e-commerce arm could change that analysis.

2. Where NCA controls can still reach you
- Government and CNI customers. Contracts with government or CNI entities usually flow ECC, and often the Third-Party Cybersecurity Controls requirements, down to suppliers. This is the most common route.
- Cloud. If you use cloud providers or sell cloud-hosted services to regulated clients, the Cloud Cybersecurity Controls (CCC-2:2024) apply to providers and tenants through those relationships.
- Sector regulators. SAMA (if you handle payments or financing), CST (telecom or IT), CMA (listed company) and the Ministry of Commerce can impose their own requirements, often aligned with NCA.
- Other NCA instruments that may become relevant: Data Cybersecurity Controls (DCC), Operational Technology Cybersecurity Controls (OTCC, for industrial or warehouse OT), Critical Systems Cybersecurity Controls (CSCC, CNI only), Telework Cybersecurity Controls (TCC), Social Media Account Cybersecurity Controls (SMACC), and the Cybersecurity Guidelines for Small and Medium Enterprises.
- Separately, the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, SDAIA) applies to you regardless of CNI status. It requires security safeguards for customer data. Retailers hold a lot of loyalty, payment and e-commerce data.
- If you accept cards, PCI DSS applies as a contractual obligation, independent of NCA.

3. Practical recommendation
- Use ECC-2:2024 as your baseline framework even though it is voluntary. It is the Saudi reference standard, it satisfies most customer questionnaires, and it maps well to ISO 27001 and NIST CSF.
- The ECC has five domains: Cybersecurity Governance, Cybersecurity Defense, Cybersecurity Resilience, Third-Party and Cloud Computing Cybersecurity, and ICS Cybersecurity. Because you are not CNI, the ICS domain is usually out of scope unless you run significant OT.
- Prioritize: governance and a board-approved cybersecurity policy, asset management, identity and access management (MFA for privileged and remote access), network and email security, vulnerability and patch management, logging and monitoring, incident response, backup and business continuity, third-party risk, cloud security, and data protection and privacy.
- Staged approach for 400 employees: gap assessment against ECC, a risk register, a 12 to 18 month roadmap, then optional independent assessment. Sector-specific (DCC) controls for customer data come next.

4. Caveats
- I cannot confirm from memory the exact control counts or any 2025 to 2026 amendments. Check the NCA website for the current version of each control set and any new applicability decisions.
