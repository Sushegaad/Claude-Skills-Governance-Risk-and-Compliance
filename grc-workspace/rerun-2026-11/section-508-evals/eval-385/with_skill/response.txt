**Section 508 incorporates WCAG 2.0, Level A and AA. As of November 2026 that isn't changing soon.**

**What is incorporated**

- The Revised Section 508 Standards and Section 255 Guidelines (36 CFR Part 1194; 82 FR 5790, January 18, 2017) incorporate WCAG 2.0 Level A and AA by reference. Compliance has been required since January 18, 2018, with a legacy ICT safe harbor.
- It applies to:
  - web content (E205.2)
  - electronic documents (E205.3)
  - software user interfaces (E205.4)
  - authoring tools (E204)
- Beyond WCAG, the standards also require the Chapter 3 functional performance criteria, the Chapter 4 hardware requirements, and Chapter 6 support documentation and services.

**Is it changing?**

No refresh rulemaking is under way as of November 2026. You may have seen reports in 2026 of a new Access Board ICT proposed rule. Those trace back to the 2015 proposed rule (80 FR 10880) and are not supported by the Federal Register record. I wouldn't cite a pending refresh. Check the Federal Register and the Access Board's website if you want to confirm.

If a refresh does come, it would go through notice-and-comment rulemaking, so there would be a proposal and a phase-in period before any new requirement applied. I can't predict whether or when that will happen.

**Where WCAG 2.1 shows up**

WCAG 2.1 is a W3C Recommendation from June 2018. It adds mobile and cognitive criteria and builds on 2.0, so a site that meets 2.1 AA also meets 2.0 AA. It is not required by Section 508. It does appear in two other rules:

- the DOJ ADA Title II web rule, for state and local governments
- the HHS Section 504 web rule, for HHS-funded entities

Both had their compliance dates extended by one year in spring 2026 (91 FR 20902 and 91 FR 25496). Check each rule for the current dates. Neither changes Section 508.

**Practical advice**

- Test and procure against WCAG 2.0 AA for 508 compliance, using a VPAT 2.x WCAG Edition ACR.
- Many organizations test against 2.1 AA as well. That's a good hedge, but it is a best practice and not a 508 requirement.
- Write contracts so they refer to "the then-current Section 508 standards." That way they don't need rewriting if the rule does change.

This is general compliance information, not legal advice.
