AI inside SOC 2 scope: the proposed criteria refresh and what applies today

Caveat: I can't verify the contents of any AICPA proposed criteria refresh. I don't have reliable detail on how a draft would treat AI, so I will not invent its wording. Please read the actual exposure draft, if one exists, and confirm its status with your auditor.

What applies to your audit today
- The 2017 Trust Services Criteria with the 2022 points of focus are the operative standard. They do not mention AI explicitly. They are technology-neutral, so your AI systems are in scope to the extent they are part of the system described in your DC 200 system description and affect your service commitments.
- The criteria are applied to AI through your existing controls. A practical mapping:
  - CC1 to CC2 (control environment, communication): AI governance roles, acceptable use policy, ethics and accountability, training.
  - CC3 (risk assessment): include AI-specific risks such as model errors, bias, hallucination, data leakage, prompt injection, and dependence on third-party model providers. Include fraud risk and change in risk from new technology.
  - CC4 (monitoring): monitoring of model performance, drift, abuse and incidents.
  - CC5 to CC6 (control activities, logical and physical access): access to models, training data, prompts, API keys, vector stores and inference endpoints. Least privilege and segregation between environments. Encryption in transit and at rest.
  - CC7 (system operations): logging, anomaly detection, incident response including AI incidents, vulnerability management for ML libraries and models.
  - CC8 (change management): model versioning, validation before release, rollback, approval for prompt or model changes, testing of fine-tuned models.
  - CC9 (risk mitigation, vendors): due diligence and contracts for LLM and AI vendors, including data use and retention terms, no training on customer data, subprocessors.
  - Confidentiality (C1): handling of customer data sent to models, retention and deletion, training data restrictions.
  - Processing Integrity (PI1): accuracy, completeness and validity of AI outputs where your commitments depend on them. Human review where appropriate.
  - Privacy (P series): purpose limitation, consent, and rights handling if personal data is used in training or inference.
  - Availability (A1): capacity and resilience of model-serving infrastructure and fallback when providers fail.
- System description: describe the AI components, data flows, third-party models as subservice organizations (carve-out or inclusive), and the commitments made about AI to customers. Make the description accurate, since auditors test what you say.

Where the proposed refresh may matter
- Any refresh is not effective until finalized. Until then it is not an audit requirement. Auditors may still ask forward-looking questions or add AI-related procedures under their own methodology, which is not the same as a criteria change.
- You can use a draft as a guide for control design, but do not represent your report as conforming to it.

What to do
1. Inventory AI systems and data flows in scope.
2. Add AI risks to your risk assessment and map controls to the existing criteria.
3. Update policies: AI acceptable use, AI vendor management, model change management, data handling.
4. Treat model providers as vendors and obtain their SOC 2 reports and subservice organization assurances.
5. Collect evidence: model change tickets, evaluation results, access reviews, monitoring alerts, vendor reviews.
6. Ask the auditor how they will test AI-related controls and whether they expect any specific description disclosures.
7. Consider complementary frameworks, for example ISO 42001 or NIST AI RMF, if customers ask for AI-specific assurance. These are not part of SOC 2.
