Here is a 2027 SOC 2 readiness timeline. It is built on the 2017 Trust Services Criteria with the 2022 Revised Points of Focus, which remain the operative criteria. The proposed 2027 criteria are tracked as a watch item and do not drive the plan.

CONTEXT THAT SHAPES THE PLAN

- On October 28, 2026, the AICPA issued an exposure draft of the proposed 2027 TSC (draft dated October 27, 2026). It keeps the five categories, consolidates overlapping criteria and adds AI-related points of focus without new AI-specific criteria.
- Comments are due January 15, 2027. Proposed effective date: engagements beginning on or after December 31, 2027, with early adoption permitted.
- So a 2027 audit period is examined against the current criteria. Controls built to them should carry forward. Do not re-map to proposed criteria.
- The separate February 2026 attestation standards draft (AT-C 105/205/210, proposed effective June 15, 2029) does not change the TSC.

ASSUMPTIONS (adjust if wrong)

- First SOC 2, with Type 1 optional, then a Type 2 with a 6-month observation window. If you already have a report, skip to the Type 2 steps.
- Scope: Security (required) plus categories your customers need.
- Audit firm is a licensed CPA firm engaged by end of Q1.

TIMELINE

Q4 2026 (now to December): decisions
- Confirm report type, categories, system boundary and target report date with leadership. Name an executive sponsor and a compliance owner.
- Shortlist and engage a CPA firm. Request their readiness approach and sample request list. Confirm they will examine against the 2017 TSC with 2022 points of focus.
- Start a gap assessment against CC1-CC9 and any chosen categories, using RAG status and remediation entries (control area, gap, remediation, owner, date, evidence needed).

January 2027
- Finish gap assessment. Prioritize the commonly flagged gaps: undocumented or unreviewed policies, no formal risk assessment, no access reviews, untested incident response plan, inconsistent change management, no vendor program.
- Kick off remediation with owners and dates.
- Optional: decide whether to comment on the exposure draft by January 15, 2027.
- Sign the audit engagement letter if not yet done.

February to March 2027: build the foundation
- Write and approve the core policy set (information security, access control, incident response, change management, risk assessment, vendor management, BC/DR, data classification, acceptable use, encryption, vulnerability management, plus privacy notice if in scope). Each one has an owner, version, approval and annual review date.
- Run the first formal risk assessment and create the risk register with owners and treatment decisions.
- Distribute policies and capture acknowledgments. Start security awareness training and background check evidence.
- Build a vendor inventory with tiers. Collect SOC 2 reports for critical vendors and map CUECs.
- Build the control matrix (ID, criterion, type, owner, frequency, evidence, test procedure).

April to May 2027: implement and operate
- Implement technical controls: MFA, SSO, endpoint protection, logging and alerting, vulnerability scanning, backup and restore tests, change approval in version control.
- Run the first access review (all in-scope systems, with documented decisions and tickets for changes). Run a first vulnerability scan cycle. Run an incident response tabletop.
- Set up automated, dated evidence exports so evidence is contemporaneous.
- If AI systems are in scope, add them to inventory, change control, vendor review and risk register now. These are low-regret under both current and proposed criteria.

June 2027: readiness and Type 1 (optional)
- Auditor-led or internal readiness review. Fix remaining gaps. Freeze control design before the observation window begins.
- Type 1 as of a date (design only), if customers need something early. Skip it if time is tight.

July to December 2027: Type 2 observation window (6 months, example)
- Monthly: evidence checks and a compliance review meeting.
- Quarterly: access review (Q3 and Q4 evidence), risk register review, vulnerability and patch reports, vendor review progress.
- Within the window: annual items (pen test, DR test, policy review, risk assessment refresh) must land inside the period.
- Sample-based evidence: changes, terminations, incidents. Keep tickets complete.
- Track and fix exceptions as they occur and document them.

Watch items during 2027
- AICPA final TSC publication: once published, ask your auditor how it affects any engagement spanning December 31, 2027 and whether early adoption is on offer. Do not adopt on your own.
- If your period starts on or after December 31, 2027, expect the new criteria to be discussed. Ask the auditor in Q3.

Q1 2028
- Auditor fieldwork, then draft and final report. Management assertion and system description reviewed by you. Plan for exceptions and management responses.
- Share the report under NDA with customers. Plan the next period to run continuously.

KEY RISKS TO THE TIMELINE
- Starting the observation window before controls have been operating long enough to be evidenced.
- Audit firm availability in Q1 2028. Book early.
- Controls on paper that do not match practice.
- Late-finding gaps in vendor and access evidence.
