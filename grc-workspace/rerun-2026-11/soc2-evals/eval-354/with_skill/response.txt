Short answer: no. The February 2026 AT-C exposure draft does not change the SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria. Your compliance manager is mixing up two separate AICPA projects.

THE TWO PROJECTS

1. Proposed 2027 SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria and Description Criteria (the TSC refresh)
   - Exposure draft issued October 28, 2026 by the AICPA's Assurance Services Executive Committee (draft dated October 27, 2026).
   - Concerns what the criteria say: the five categories are kept, overlapping criteria are consolidated, and AI-related points of focus are added (no new AI-specific criteria).
   - Comments due January 15, 2027. Proposed effective for engagements beginning on or after December 31, 2027, with early adoption permitted.

2. Attestation standards exposure draft on AT-C 105, 205 and 210 (February 2026)
   - Concerns the standards an auditor follows when performing attestation engagements: how the CPA performs and reports on the work.
   - Proposed effective date: June 15, 2029.
   - It does not change the TSC. The criteria that you are measured against are a different set of text.

A simple way to explain it: the TSC is the yardstick (the criteria for security, availability and so on), while AT-C is the rulebook for how the CPA measures with it. Changing one does not change the other.

WHAT APPLIES TO YOU NOW

- Until the AICPA adopts a final TSC refresh, the 2017 TSC with the 2022 Revised Points of Focus are the operative criteria. Current engagements are unaffected.
- The AT-C draft is proposed for 2029 and is not in force either. Nothing in it requires action from you now.

WHAT TO DO

1. Correct the internal record. Note in your compliance plan that there are two separate items with separate dates (December 31, 2027 for the proposed TSC, June 15, 2029 for the proposed AT-C), and that only the TSC project affects your control criteria.
2. Ask your auditor which document they meant when they mentioned new criteria. Most likely it is the October 2026 TSC draft.
3. Keep your control framework mapped to the current criteria (CC1-CC9, A1, C1, PI1, P1-P8).
4. The AT-C changes are for the audit firm to absorb. You may see changes in engagement letters or reporting language in future years, but this is not a control matter.
5. Optionally, comment on the TSC draft by January 15, 2027.

Your licensed CPA firm can confirm the status of both drafts for your engagement. This is general guidance, not legal advice.
