US banking TPRM guidance: status as of November 2026

Short answer: the June 2023 Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships (OCC Bulletin 2023-17) is still the operative framework. A proposed replacement is out for comment. It is a proposal only and should not be applied as if it were final.

1. The proposal
- On September 15, 2026 the OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC and NCUA published "Proposed Third-Party Risk Management Guidance" in the Federal Register (91 FR 58536).
- It would rescind and replace the June 2023 Interagency Guidance. NCUA would join the interagency guidance for the first time.
- It would also rescind three other documents: the OCC's foreign-based third-party bulletin, the 2024 bank-fintech joint statement, and the 2024 community bank TPRM guide.
- Comments are due November 16, 2026. That is about ten days from today (November 6), so if you want to influence the final text, act now.
- The agencies also issued a Joint Statement on Community Banks' Engagement with Core Service Providers. Community banks and credit unions should read it, especially if they depend heavily on one core provider.

2. What this means today
- The June 2023 guidance stays in force until a final replacement is issued. Examiners will keep assessing you against it.
- Do not rebuild the programme around the proposed text. It could change before it is finalised, and the effective date and transition terms are not yet known.
- The 2023 lifecycle is still the structure to use: planning, due diligence and selection, contract negotiation, ongoing monitoring, termination. Rigor should be proportional to risk and criticality.
- The documents the proposal would rescind (the foreign-based third-party bulletin, the bank-fintech statement, the community bank guide) are still current for now. If your programme relies on them, keep following them until rescission takes effect.

3. Recommended actions
1. Read the Federal Register text and compare it with your current policy. Do a gap analysis against the 2023 lifecycle stages, and mark which gaps are proposal-driven and which are existing exposures.
2. Decide whether to submit a comment by November 16, 2026. Focus on points that affect your institution, such as proportionality for community banks, fintech arrangements, and critical activities.
3. Open a tracking item in your regulatory change process. Name an owner, record the comment deadline, and set a trigger to re-baseline when a final rule or guidance is published.
4. Keep strengthening the fundamentals that every version of the guidance is likely to expect:
   - A current vendor inventory with risk tiers. Tier by data access, criticality and substitutability.
   - Fourth-party and concentration visibility.
   - Contracts that carry audit rights, incident notification, sub-processor flow-down and termination assistance.
   - Documented ongoing monitoring, board reporting, and periodic independent review of the programme itself.
   - Tested exit plans for critical vendors.
5. If you have EU operations, DORA (Chapter V, Arts. 28-30) is a separate and already-applicable regime. Do not conflate it with the US proposal.

4. Related note for Irish entities
The Central Bank of Ireland has its own consultation, CP173, which closes December 3, 2026. It is separate from the US process.

Caveat: this is general compliance information, not legal advice. Check the Federal Register notice and your primary regulator's communications. Confirm current status before relying on it, since a final action could be issued after my information was verified (November 6, 2026).
