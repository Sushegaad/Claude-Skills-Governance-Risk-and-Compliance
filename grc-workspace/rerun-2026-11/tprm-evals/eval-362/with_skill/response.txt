What changed in October 2026 for Irish regulated entities on outsourcing and third-party risk

Short answer: on October 8, 2026 the Central Bank of Ireland (CBI) published CP173, "Consultation on Guidance on Good Practices for the Sound Management of Third-Party Risks". It is a consultation, not final guidance. Your current obligations have not changed yet.

Key facts
- Published: October 8, 2026. The consultation closes December 3, 2026.
- Purpose: the final guidance will replace the December 2021 Cross-Industry Guidance on Outsourcing. That 2021 guidance is still the reference point until the final version is issued.
- Approach: outcomes-based. It covers "third-party risks" broadly, not only classic outsourcing.
- Context: it follows the EBA's September 2026 final guidelines on third-party risk for non-ICT services. The CBI proposal appears to be part of the same move toward a broader third-party risk lens beyond ICT.

What it means in practice
1. Keep complying with the 2021 Cross-Industry Guidance on Outsourcing. Do not treat CP173 as in force.
2. Use the consultation window. Read CP173 in full, map it against your outsourcing policy, register and contracts, and decide whether to respond before December 3, 2026.
3. Prepare for scope widening. An outcomes-based, third-party-wide approach usually means that non-ICT and non-outsourcing arrangements will be pulled into your governance. Check whether your inventory and tiering cover all third-party arrangements, and not only formal outsourcing.
4. Consider the EBA non-ICT guidelines if you are a credit institution or another entity in their scope. Check their scope and application date against your entity type.
5. If you are a financial entity under DORA, that regime applies since January 17, 2025 and is separate from CP173. Chapter V remains in force:
   - Art. 28: ICT third-party strategy and the Register of Information.
   - Art. 29: concentration risk, including substitutability.
   - Art. 30: mandatory contract provisions.
   Run one programme with many citations. Apply the strictest requirement as the baseline.

Suggested actions
- Name an owner and put the CBI consultation, its response deadline and a re-baseline trigger in your regulatory change log.
- Gap-assess your programme against CP173 once you have read it. Look at inventory completeness, criticality assessment, due diligence, contract content, ongoing monitoring, exit planning, fourth-party visibility and board oversight.
- Brief your board and risk committee on the direction of travel.
- Do not rewrite policies yet. Wait for the final text, and prepare a plan so that you can move quickly once it is published.

Caveat: I have not reproduced detailed CP173 provisions because I cannot verify them here. Read the CBI paper itself for specifics such as proposed expectations, proportionality and transition. This is general compliance information, not legal advice.
