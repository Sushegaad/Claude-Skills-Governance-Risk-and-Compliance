DORA major-incident reporting (Art. 19 of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554). The timings are set in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301 (the RTS on content and time limits), and the templates are in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/302. Classification of "major" follows Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1772.

Deadlines for a major ICT-related incident:
1. Initial notification: within 4 hours of classifying the incident as major, and no later than 24 hours from the moment the entity became aware of the incident. The 24-hour limit is a backstop, so classification must be done promptly.
2. Intermediate report: within 72 hours of the initial notification (or of the previous report). It must be updated without undue delay whenever the situation changes materially, and an update is also due when regular activities have recovered. An intermediate report is also required if the status changes materially.
3. Final report: within 1 month of the latest updated intermediate report, once root cause analysis is done, whether or not mitigation has finished. If the incident is still ongoing at that point, a status update is made first and the final report follows within 1 month of resolution.

Do weekends count? In principle yes. The clocks run in elapsed hours and are not business-day clocks, so an incident classified on a Saturday still triggers the 4-hour initial notification. The RTS does give limited relief for weekends and bank holidays. As I recall, it applies for the intermediate and final reports, and it applies to financial entities that are not systemically significant types (for example, it does not cover credit institutions, central counterparties, trading venues or other entities that operate across the weekend). Where it applies, the due date moves to the next working day. I am not certain of the exact scope, so check the final text of Art. 5 of Delegated Regulation 2025/301 and your competent authority's guidance. Plan on 24/7 coverage for the initial notice.

Related duties: significant cyber threats may be notified voluntarily (Art. 19(2)). Where the incident affects clients' financial interests, entities must inform clients without undue delay (Art. 19(3)). Reports go to the competent authority, and from there to the ESAs, ECB and others (Art. 19(6)-(7)).
