Major-incident reporting is under Art. 19 DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554), with content and timing in CDR (EU) 2025/301 and templates in CIR (EU) 2025/302. Classification as major follows Art. 18 and CDR (EU) 2024/1772, where meeting any single threshold makes an incident major.

Deadlines
| Stage | Deadline | Clock starts |
|---|---|---|
| Initial notification | Within 4 hours of classification as major, and no later than 24 hours from the entity becoming aware of the incident | Classification as major, with a 24-hour backstop from awareness |
| Intermediate report | Within 72 hours from submission of the initial notification | Submission of the initial notification |
| Final report | No later than 1 month from the (latest) intermediate report | Submission of the (latest) intermediate report |

Notes on the clocks
- The 4-hour clock runs from classification, not detection. Do not delay classification: prolonged triage is itself a supervisory risk under Art. 17(1). Classify as soon as any threshold is met and do not wait for root cause.
- Updated intermediate reports are required when the situation changes materially. The final report is due after root cause analysis is complete.
- Reports go to the home Member State competent authority, which forwards them to host authorities (Art. 19(5)).
- Payment-related incidents follow Art. 23 and the dedicated payment template in CIR (EU) 2025/302.

Do weekends count?
- Generally the clocks run continuously. They are not business-hour clocks, so a Friday-evening major incident still has a 4-hour and 24-hour deadline.
- Relief: where a deadline falls on a weekend or bank holiday, submission may be made by noon of the next working day.
- Exception: this relief is disapplied for the initial and intermediate reports of significant credit institutions. They must meet the standard deadlines regardless of weekends or bank holidays.
- Plan for a 24/7 reporting capability: an on-call incident commander, pre-populated templates and an escalation chain to senior management and the board (Art. 17(3)).

Also consider voluntary notification of significant cyber threats (Art. 19(2)), and notify affected clients without undue delay (Art. 17(4)).
