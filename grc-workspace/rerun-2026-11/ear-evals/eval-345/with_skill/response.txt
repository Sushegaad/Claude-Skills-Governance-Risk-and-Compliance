SHORT ANSWER
Below is a gap table for the seven elements of an export compliance program, updated for the Affiliates Rule (90 FR 47201). The rule extends Entity List and MEU List license requirements (and certain SDN-related requirements) to entities 50 percent or more owned, directly or indirectly, individually or in aggregate, by listed parties. It was stayed from November 10, 2025 (90 FR 50857) and the stay was extended by FR Doc 2026-22793 (effective November 9, 2026) to January 10, 2027, with no substantive changes. Make the program ready before January 10, 2027, and do not plan on another extension.

GAP TABLE
Element | Update needed | Priority | Action and owner

1. Management commitment | Leadership must own a new, material risk that cannot be solved by a name-match tool. | High | Brief the board or senior officer and get written sign-off on an Affiliates Rule readiness plan with a go-live date and budget (ownership data, headcount). Add the rule to the next ECP certification and to quarterly reporting. Owner: CCO or export control officer (ECO).

2. Risk assessment | Identify the exposure. Most customers will not be affected, but the ones that are will be hard to see. | High | Map customers, distributors, end-users, consignees and banks by country and sector. Flag those with opaque ownership, with links to Entity List or MEU List parties, or located in high-risk jurisdictions (China and Hong Kong, Russia, transshipment hubs). Identify open orders, backlog and long-lead contracts that could hit the cutoff. Re-examine the licensing-exception reliance for those parties, since exceptions are generally unavailable once captured. Owner: ECO with sales operations.

3. Written policies and procedures | Policies assume named-party matching. | High | Revise the screening, onboarding, order acceptance and distributor procedures to require ownership analysis: collect direct and indirect owners, calculate aggregate holdings by listed parties (for example, 30% + 30% = 60% is covered), apply the 50 percent threshold, and escalate unknowns. Define a hit protocol: stop shipment, escalate to ECO and counsel, determine whether a license is required, decide on apply or refuse, document. Add an "effective-date switch" that turns on the rule on January 10, 2027 (or on any BIS change). Add a procedure for monitoring BIS notices so a further stay or change is acted on quickly. Owner: ECO and legal.

4. Training and awareness | Sales, onboarding, finance and logistics need to understand ownership-based restrictions. | Medium | Run role-based training before year-end: sales (awareness and ownership red flags), onboarding and finance (data collection and aggregation), ECO and legal (deep dive). Record sign-offs and keep records for 5 years. Include examples such as the aggregate 60 percent case and the Plexon lesson that research and medical customers are not exempt. Owner: ECO and HR/training.

5. Restricted party screening | The current tool only checks named Entity List matches. This is the central gap. | Critical | Add ownership-based screening: obtain ownership data for all parties, calculate indirect and aggregate holdings, and screen owners against the Entity List, MEU List, Unverified List, Denied Persons List, SDN and the Consolidated Screening List. Confirm with the vendor whether it supports affiliates and aggregation, or add a corporate-family-tree data source. Back-screen the entire active customer and distributor base before January 10, 2027. Keep re-screening at least monthly and on any change of party or ownership. Flag 25 to 49 percent holdings by listed parties for watch. Keep dated screening and ownership evidence. Owner: ECO and IT or compliance operations.

6. Due diligence (know your customer) | Ownership certification is not currently standard. | High | Add an ownership certification and an organization chart to onboarding and recertification, including an obligation to notify you of ownership change. Put the following in contracts: a representation that no listed party owns 50 percent or more, notification, and rights to suspend or terminate. Extend to distributors and sub-distributors in D:1 and other high-risk territories, with audit rights. Treat refusal or inability to disclose ownership as a red flag (§ 732.6 and Supplement 3 to Part 732). Owner: sales, legal and ECO.

7. Recordkeeping and audits | Ownership analysis is a new record class. | Medium | Retain ownership evidence, calculations, screening results and decisions for 5 years (§ 762.6). Add the Affiliates Rule to the annual internal audit and plan a third-party assessment for high-risk exporters. Test the procedure with sample cases (direct 50 percent, indirect through a holding company, aggregate of two listed parties). Document any VSD decision: if you find apparent past violations, consider a voluntary self-disclosure under § 764.5. Owner: ECO and internal audit.

TIMELINE
- Now to mid-December: complete the ownership data gap assessment, vendor decision, and procedure drafts.
- Mid-December to January 10, 2027: complete back-screening, training, contract amendments and order-hold decisions. Test the process.
- January 10, 2027: stay lapses. Ownership screening goes live. Monitor the Federal Register for any further action.

CAVEATS
This is general compliance information, not legal advice. Confirm the status of the stay and any amendment in the Federal Register, and use counsel for complex ownership structures, such as control without majority ownership, which the 50 percent test does not cover but which remains a red flag.
