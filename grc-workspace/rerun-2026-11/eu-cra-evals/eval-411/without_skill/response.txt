Classification under the Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847), using the Annex III (important, Class I and II) and Annex IV (critical) lists, as detailed in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392.

1. Password manager: Important product, Class I (Annex III, Part I). Password managers are listed explicitly.
2. Hypervisor: Important product, Class II (Annex III, Part II). The entry is "hypervisors and container runtime systems that support virtualised execution of operating systems and similar environments".
3. Smart meter gateway: Critical product (Annex IV). The entry is "smart meter gateways within smart metering systems as defined in Directive (EU) 2019/944 and other devices for advanced security purposes, including for secure cryptoprocessing". Critical is the highest tier. In practice the Commission can require an EU cybersecurity certificate (for example under EUCC) at least at "substantial" assurance, once it adopts a delegated act under Art. 8(1). Until then, the manufacturer uses the Annex VIII conformity routes (third-party Module B+C or Module H, or applicable harmonised standards, common specifications or certification). Sector rules may also apply.
4. Industrial robot: Not listed in Annex III or IV, so it falls in the default category. Conformity is by internal control (Module A) self-assessment. It is a product with digital elements if it has a data connection. Note the interplay with the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230. The Machinery Regulation has its own cybersecurity-related essential requirements. Where it is a high-risk AI system under the AI Act, CRA Art. 12 lets compliance with the CRA Annex I essential requirements count towards the AI Act cybersecurity requirement, subject to conditions. Reassess if the robot includes embedded components that are themselves listed, such as a secure element.

Caveat: classification depends on the product's core functionality, per the technical descriptions in the Implementing Regulation. Confirm against that text for borderline cases.
