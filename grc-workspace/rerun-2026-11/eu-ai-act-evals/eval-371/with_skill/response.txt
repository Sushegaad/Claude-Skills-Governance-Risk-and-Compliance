Short answer: yes. Enforcement has started, but it is at the information-gathering stage. I am not aware of any public fine or formal non-compliance decision against a GPAI provider as of 6 November 2026.

Legal basis
- The Commission's (AI Office) enforcement powers over GPAI model providers have applied since 2 August 2026. They cover information requests (Art. 91), model evaluations (Art. 92), requests for mitigation measures (Art. 93), and fines (Art. 101) of up to EUR 15M or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.
- The substantive GPAI obligations (Arts. 53-55) have applied since 2 August 2025. Between August 2025 and August 2026 the AI Office could supervise but could not use these coercive powers.

What happened in 2026
- 2 Aug 2026: enforcement powers switched on.
- 10 Aug 2026: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1755 entered into force. It sets the procedural rules for Commission proceedings against GPAI providers (published in the OJ on 21 July 2026).
- Late August 2026: the first formal Art. 91 requests for information to GPAI providers were reported.
- 12 Oct 2026: consultation opened on a further draft implementing act on procedural rules for penalty proceedings. It closes 9 November 2026.
- 14 Oct 2026: the AI Office announced a further wave of Art. 91 requests. It covers security practices, external evaluation, and post-market monitoring.
- 15 Oct 2026: EN 18286:2026 was cited in the OJEU. It is a quality-management standard and relates to high-risk systems (Art. 17), not directly to GPAI duties.

Caveats
- The recipients of the requests have not been officially named. Treat press attribution to specific companies as unconfirmed.
- Reporting on the first wave comes from secondary sources. The public record does not show the full content of the requests.
- The October wave topics (security, external evaluation, post-market monitoring) are most relevant to systemic-risk models, where Art. 55 requires evaluation and red-teaming, cybersecurity protection, and serious-incident tracking.
- A request is not a finding of breach. Under Reg. 2026/1755 a fine would normally follow preliminary findings, a right to be heard, and access to the file, and there is a five-year limitation period. Fines are therefore unlikely to arrive quickly.

What this means if you are a GPAI provider
1. Assume your documentation will be inspected. Annex XI technical documentation, Annex XII downstream information, the copyright policy (Directive 2019/790 Art. 4(3) opt-outs), and the public training-content summary should be current and producible quickly.
2. Systemic-risk models (presumed above 10^25 FLOPs, or designated by the Commission) must have their Safety and Security Framework in place, with red-teaming evidence, incident processes, and cybersecurity controls. Confirm that you notified the AI Office within 2 weeks of crossing the threshold.
3. The Code of Practice is the main route to a presumption of compliance. There are 22 full signatories as of the Commission page updated 7 October 2026, and xAI signed only the Safety and Security chapter. Non-signatories need an equivalent, documented alternative demonstration.
4. Legacy models (placed on the market before 2 Aug 2025) have until 2 August 2027 to comply fully. The Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking requirement for models placed before 2 Aug 2026 is due 2 December 2026.
5. Set up a response procedure for Art. 91 requests: a named owner, a legal-privilege and trade-secret review, a fast evidence-retrieval path, and consistent answers across the whole organisation.
6. Misleading or incomplete answers to authorities carry their own exposure, up to EUR 7.5M or 1% of turnover (Art. 99(5)).

Bottom line: enforcement is real but still in the supervisory phase. The risk to manage now is being unable to respond credibly to an information request, not an imminent fine.

This is general compliance information, not legal advice. Check the Commission and AI Office pages for any update after early November 2026.
