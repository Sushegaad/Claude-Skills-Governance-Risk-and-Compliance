Status as of November 2026: the harmonised standards programme is late and only just beginning to produce results. One standard is cited in the Official Journal. Most of what high-risk providers need is still pending.

What exists
- EN 18286:2026 (AI quality management system, supporting Art. 17) became the first AI Act harmonised standard cited in the OJEU. It was announced on 15 October 2026.
- prEN 18228 (risk management, supporting Art. 9) is in final approval but not yet cited.
- The deadline for the broader standardisation request was revised to February 2027.

Why the delay matters less than it once did
- Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026) moved the high-risk dates. Annex III standalone systems now apply from 2 December 2027, and Annex I embedded-product systems from 2 August 2028. That gives standards bodies and providers more time.

What it means for conformity
1. Presumption of conformity (Art. 40). Applying a cited harmonised standard gives a presumption that you meet the corresponding requirements. Today that covers only the Art. 17 quality management system, and only if you apply EN 18286:2026 fully. It does not cover Arts. 9, 10, 12-15, so you must still show conformity with those by other means: your own documented methods, common specifications if adopted, or ISO/IEC-based frameworks used as evidence.
2. Presumption is not safe harbour. It is rebuttable, and it covers only the requirements the standard addresses. You still need a risk management system, data governance, technical documentation, logging, transparency, oversight, and accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity.
3. Conformity assessment route (Art. 43).
 - Annex III points 2-8: internal control (Annex VI) only, so the lack of standards does not require a notified body. You need to document how you meet each requirement without a presumption.
 - Annex III point 1 (biometrics): a notified body (Annex VII) is mandatory if you have not applied harmonised standards, or applied them only in part, or none exist. In practice, until standards for Arts. 9-15 are cited, biometric providers should plan on a notified body.
 - Annex I products: the sectoral conformity procedure applies.
4. QMS now. If you build a QMS (13 components in Art. 17), design it to EN 18286:2026 now. ISO/IEC 42001 certification helps as supporting evidence but does not by itself give the presumption.
5. Documentation. Record which standards you applied (Annex IV requires a list of harmonised standards applied, or a description of alternative solutions). Keep a gap register against each article so you can switch to the standards as they are cited.

Recommended actions
- Adopt EN 18286:2026 for the QMS.
- Track the cited-standards list in the OJEU and prEN 18228 progress.
- Build risk management to the Art. 9 text and map to ISO 42001 and NIST AI RMF (MAP/MEASURE) as interim evidence.
- Biometric providers: start notified-body engagement early.
- Do not wait for standards to start. The December 2027 date is later but not far off for technical documentation and testing.

This is general compliance information, not legal advice. Confirm current OJEU citation status before relying on a presumption.
