Determining whether you're an Essential Entity (EE) or Important Entity (IE) under NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555) requires running three tests in sequence — sector, size, and the essential/important split — because Annex I sector membership alone does NOT make you essential; size and designation both matter, and misclassification is the single most common and costly NIS2 error. I don't have your sector, headcount, turnover, or balance sheet, so I'll walk through the framework and flag exactly what I need from you to reach a conclusion, plus what the classification changes in practice either way.

**Step 1 — Sector scope (Annex I vs. Annex II).** Annex I (high-criticality) covers energy, transport, banking, financial market infrastructure, health, drinking water, waste water, digital infrastructure (DNS, cloud, data centres, CDNs, trust services, public electronic communications), ICT service management B2B (MSP/MSSP), public administration, and space. Annex II (other critical) covers postal/courier, waste management, chemicals, food, manufacturing (medical devices, electronics, machinery, motor vehicles), digital providers (marketplaces, search engines, social platforms), and research organisations. If you offer B2B SaaS, note that this generally qualifies as a "cloud computing service" under Art. 6(30) and lands you in Annex I digital infrastructure regardless of how you market it — analyse the actual service model, not the label.

**Step 2 — Size threshold (Art. 2(1), SME Recommendation 2003/361).** You're in scope at all only if you're medium-sized or larger: ≥50 employees, OR annual turnover and balance sheet total both above €10M. Below that, you're generally out of scope — except for a hard-coded list that's in scope regardless of size: qualified trust service providers, TLD registries and DNS providers, sole providers of a critical service in a Member State, entities whose disruption could have significant public-safety/systemic cross-border impact, central government public administration, and any entity a Member State specifically designates.

**Step 3 — Essential vs. Important (Art. 3).** You're an Essential Entity if you're in an Annex I sector AND exceed the large-enterprise ceiling: ≥250 employees, OR turnover >€50M together with balance sheet >€43M. You're also automatically essential regardless of size if you're a qualified trust service provider, TLD registry, DNS provider, a medium-or-larger public electronic communications network/service provider, central government public administration, or an entity designated critical under the CER Directive (EU) 2022/2557 (CER designation flows automatically into essential-entity status under NIS2 Art. 3(1)(f)). Everything else in scope — medium-sized Annex I entities, and all in-scope Annex II entities — defaults to Important Entity, unless your Member State specifically designates you essential.

A worked example that catches people out: a company with 200 employees and €50M turnover in an Annex I sector is in scope (clears the medium threshold) but does not clear the large-enterprise ceiling (needs ≥250 employees, or turnover strictly above €50M plus balance sheet above €43M) — so it defaults to **Important**, not Essential, unless national designation (e.g., under a Member State's specific critical-infrastructure thresholds) or CER designation pushes it up.

**Why the answer matters — the practical consequences either way:**

| | Essential Entity | Important Entity |
|---|---|---|
| Supervision | Ex-ante *and* ex-post: proactive on-site inspections, regular/targeted/ad-hoc security audits, security scans, evidence requests (Art. 32) | Ex-post only: triggered by evidence or indication of non-compliance (Art. 33) |
| Enforcement | Warnings, binding instructions, remediation orders, audit orders, public disclosure orders, and ultimately temporary suspension of certification/authorisation or of managerial duties | Warnings, binding instructions, remediation orders, audit orders — no suspension power |
| Maximum fine (Art. 34) | Higher of €10,000,000 or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover | Higher of €7,000,000 or 1.4% of total worldwide annual turnover |
| Management liability | Personal liability, with possible temporary ban from managerial functions | Personal liability |

Importantly, the substantive obligations are identical regardless of classification: both tiers must meet the Art. 20 governance requirements (management body approval, oversight, and training) and the Art. 21(2) ten-measure risk-management programme, and both face the same Art. 23 incident-reporting clock (24h/72h/1-month). Classification changes how proactively you'll be supervised and your penalty ceiling — not whether the underlying security programme is required.
