Background on the Dutch transposition situation: NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555) had a transposition deadline of 17 October 2024, which the Netherlands missed, along with a majority of other member states. The Dutch implementing law is the "Cyberbeveiligingswet" (Cyber Security Act, Cbw), which has gone through a legislative process (Raad van State advice, parliamentary consultation, Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer review) well into 2025 and, based on the pattern of delay, plausibly into 2026. What actually matters for a logistics operator in scope of NIS2 in the Netherlands:

- Confirm your classification. Freight transport, logistics, and supply chain management services are listed under NIS2 Annex I (Transport sector) and can be essential or important entities depending on size and criticality. Some "critical" designations (e.g., single points of failure in national logistics) can pull in even smaller entities regardless of the standard size thresholds.
- Registration obligation: NIS2 requires entities to register with the relevant competent authority/CSIRT within a set window after the law's national entry into force, providing details like legal name, sector, contact points, IP ranges, and member states of operation. If the Cbw or its decree became binding in August 2026, this is very likely the operative deadline you're now facing.
- Governance duty (Article 20): management bodies must approve cybersecurity risk-management measures and undergo training; management can be held personally liable for non-compliance.
- Article 21 technical/organisational measures: risk analysis, incident handling, business continuity/backup, supply chain security, vulnerability handling, cryptography/encryption, access control, and use of multi-factor authentication.
- Incident reporting: 24-hour early warning, 72-hour incident notification, and a final report within one month to the Dutch CSIRT/competent authority.
