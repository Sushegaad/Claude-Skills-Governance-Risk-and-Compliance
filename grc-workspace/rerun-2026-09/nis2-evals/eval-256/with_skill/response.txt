You operate critical logistics infrastructure in the Netherlands, so the change that matters most is national, not EU-level: on 15 August 2026 the Cyberbeveiligingswet (Cbw) entered into force, alongside the Wet weerbaarheid kritieke entiteiten (Wwke), replacing the previous Wet beveiliging netwerk- en informatiesystemen (Wbni). This is the Netherlands' NIS2 transposition act, and it applies immediately — there is no transition period. Roughly 8,000 organisations across 18 sectors fall in scope, and transport/logistics is one of the Annex I high-criticality sectors under NIS2 itself (Directive (EU) 2022/2555), so if your logistics operation was previously monitoring NIS2 only as an EU-level directive with no binding force in the Netherlands, that gap is now closed.

What changed in practice, effective 15 August 2026:

1. **Registration is live and mandatory.** In-scope entities must register with the national authority via MijnNCSC (the Dutch National Cyber Security Centre's portal). If you have not registered, this is the most urgent action item — do it now, not after a compliance review.

2. **Duty of care (zorgplicht) applies now.** This is the Dutch implementation of NIS2 Art. 21's ten risk-management measures — risk analysis and policies, incident handling, business continuity/backup/DR, supply chain security, secure acquisition/development, effectiveness assessment, cyber hygiene and training, cryptography, HR/access control/asset management, and MFA/secured communications. If your risk-management programme was still "NIS2-ready" rather than "NIS2-implemented," it needs to be operational immediately — the Cbw does not give you a grace period to build toward it.

3. **Incident reporting obligations apply now**, on the Art. 23 clock: a 24-hour early warning, a 72-hour incident notification, and a final report within one month, all measured from the moment your organisation becomes aware of a significant incident. You should confirm your incident response plan has a pre-drafted notification path to the Dutch CSIRT (or the relevant sectoral CSIRT) and that "awareness" is defined clearly enough internally that the clock-start isn't ambiguous during a live incident.

4. **Board accountability applies now.** Under the Cbw (mirroring NIS2 Art. 20), your management body must approve the risk-management measures, oversee their implementation, and undergo cybersecurity training itself — not just mandate it for staff. Management can face personal liability for infringements. If you don't already have board-level approval minutes and a standing cybersecurity oversight agenda item, generate that evidence now; it's the artifact regulators and auditors will ask for first.

A classification nuance specific to logistics: confirm whether your entity sits in Annex I (transport: air, rail, water, road) or is itself designated as a critical entity under the CER Directive (EU) 2022/2557, since CER-designated entities are automatically essential entities under NIS2 Art. 3(1)(f) — that carries ex-ante supervision (Art. 32: proactive inspections, audits, security scans) rather than the ex-post, complaint/evidence-triggered supervision (Art. 33) that applies to important entities. This distinction should already be reflected in how the Dutch authority has classified you under Cbw/Wwke; if you haven't received or confirmed that classification, that's a call to make to the NCSC or your sector regulator this week, since it determines both your penalty exposure (Art. 34: essential entities face fines up to the higher of €10M or 2% of global turnover; important entities up to the higher of €7M or 1.4%) and how proactively you should expect to be audited.

Practical next steps, in priority order: (1) confirm MijnNCSC registration status and complete it if outstanding; (2) confirm your essential-vs-important classification under the Cbw/Wwke, including any CER designation; (3) verify your incident response plan maps cleanly onto the 24h/72h/1-month timeline with a defined notification channel; (4) produce or refresh board-level approval and training evidence for the risk-management measures; (5) if you have EU operations outside NL, note that France, Ireland and Spain remain behind on transposition and were referred to the CJEU in July 2026 — build your group-wide programme to the strictest transposed standard (currently the Dutch Cbw) rather than waiting on the laggards. This is general compliance guidance, not legal advice — confirm specifics with NCSC-NL or Dutch counsel given the immediate, no-transition-period applicability.
