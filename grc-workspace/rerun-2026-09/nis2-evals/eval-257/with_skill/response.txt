As of September 2026, based on the most recent enforcement action I can confirm: on 9 July 2026 the European Commission referred Ireland, Spain, France, and the Netherlands to the CJEU for failing to notify transposition of NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555), seeking a lump-sum penalty plus daily fines. Of that group, the Netherlands has since transposed — its Cyberbeveiligingswet (Cbw), together with the Wet weerbaarheid kritieke entiteiten (Wwke), entered into force on 15 August 2026, replacing the prior Wbni, with no transition period.

That leaves Ireland, Spain, and France — the three jurisdictions where you have subsidiaries — as still lacking in-force national transposition as of the CJEU referral date. I want to flag directly that I should not assert their current status from memory beyond that referral: transposition bills can move quickly, and it's plausible one or more of these states has since passed implementing legislation between July and September 2026 without that reaching my knowledge. Before you finalize any compliance timeline, verify current status directly with each national authority: the NCSC in Ireland, INCIBE/the Spanish national cybersecurity framework authorities in Spain, and ANSSI in France. Each has published or will publish draft/final implementing law and a registration portal; check those directly rather than relying on secondary sources.

What this means practically for a group with subsidiaries in all three:

**The directive still binds you even without transposition.** NIS2 is a directive, meaning its legal force runs through national law, but that does not mean you are exempt until France, Ireland, or Spain formally transpose. Two things are true simultaneously: (1) individual entities generally cannot be fined under national law until that law exists and applies to them, but (2) once a national act does land, historical practice across other Member States (Germany, Belgium, Italy, etc., which transposed earlier) has been to apply short or no compliance windows — the Netherlands' Cbw is the most recent example, with zero transition period. Waiting until a French, Irish, or Spanish law is signed to start building your programme is a bad bet; you would likely be racing an already-live compliance deadline.

**Build to the directive text now, not to the weakest national variant.** For a multi-country group, best practice is: (1) classify each subsidiary's sector and size under NIS2 Art. 2/Art. 3 as if the directive applied directly — Annex I/Annex II sector scope, the ≥50 employee or >€10M turnover/balance-sheet threshold for in-scope status, and the ≥250 employee or >€50M turnover with >€43M balance sheet threshold for essential-entity status; (2) implement the Art. 21(2) ten-measure risk-management programme and Art. 20 governance obligations (management body approval, oversight, training, personal liability exposure) group-wide, since these substantive obligations are unlikely to be watered down in any national transposition; (3) build an Art. 23 incident-reporting capability (24h early warning, 72h notification, 1-month final report) with defined national CSIRT reporting channels for each subsidiary — pending official channels, use the National Coordination Centres or interim contact points these Member States tend to publish ahead of full legal transposition; (4) apply the strictest currently-transposed national variant as your group baseline — right now that is the Dutch Cbw, since it's the most detailed live implementation among comparable Member States — until France, Ireland, and Spain publish their own.

**Watch the CJEU referral for signal, not for your deadline.** The July 2026 referral pressures these states with financial penalties for the *state*, not directly for your subsidiaries, but it's a strong indicator these transpositions are imminent (likely under active legislative or executive drafting). Treat "not yet transposed" as "transposing soon, with limited notice," and keep a standing watch on the national gazette/official journal in each jurisdiction plus ANSSI/NCSC/INCIBE bulletins.

Bottom line: confirm current Irish, Spanish, and French transposition status directly with those authorities before relying on any snapshot including this one, but proceed now as if NIS2's substantive obligations (Arts. 20, 21, 23) apply to your subsidiaries in all three, since that is both the safer and, most likely, the accurate posture.
