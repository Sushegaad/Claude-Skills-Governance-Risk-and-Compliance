I can't give you a definitive, current-as-of-September-2026 list of which member states have or haven't transposed NIS2, and I'd rather flag that clearly than guess at a status that may have changed. What I can offer is the pattern of delay that was well-established through 2024-2025, plus specific notes on Ireland, Spain, and France, so your team can verify the live status against the European Commission's infringement tracker and each country's official gazette.

The general picture: NIS2's transposition deadline was 17 October 2024. The European Commission opened infringement proceedings (formal notices under Article 258 TFEU) against roughly two-thirds of member states shortly after that deadline for failing to fully transpose or notify transposition measures. By early-to-mid 2025, only a small handful of states (commonly cited: Belgium, Croatia, Italy, Lithuania, and a few others) had substantially completed transposition, while most of the EU-27 were still working through national legislation. Given that pattern, it would not be surprising if some member states were still finalizing secondary legislation (implementing decrees, sector-specific regulations, designation of competent authorities) well into 2026, even if the primary transposition law had passed.

On your three specific jurisdictions:

Ireland: Ireland has historically been one of the slower movers on NIS-related transposition (it was also late on the original NIS Directive). The National Cyber Security Bill has been the vehicle for NIS2 transposition, with the NCSC (National Cyber Security Centre) expected to be designated as the national competent authority/single point of contact. As of the information I have, I cannot confirm whether this bill had been enacted and commenced by September 2026 — you should check the Irish Department of the Environment, Climate and Communications and the Oireachtas legislative tracker directly.

Spain: Spain has been working through a draft "Ley de coordinación y gobernanza de la ciberseguridad" to transpose NIS2, with INCIBE (Instituto Nacional de Ciberseguridad) expected to play a central operational role and CCN-CERT/CNPIC also involved given overlap with existing critical infrastructure protection law (Ley PIC). Draft versions circulated through 2024-2025 but I don't have confirmed data on final enactment status by September 2026.

France: France has generally moved somewhat faster on cyber regulation transposition given ANSSI's established role, using a project of law ("projet de loi relatif à la résilience des infrastructures critiques et au renforcement de la cybersécurité," sometimes referred to covering both NIS2 and the CER Directive) to transpose both directives together. There were reports of this advancing through the French Parliament in 2024-2025, but again, I cannot confirm final enactment or full entry into force by September 2026 without checking a current source.

Why this matters practically for a group with subsidiaries in these three states: NIS2 is a minimum-harmonization directive, meaning each member state can gold-plate requirements (stricter incident reporting timelines, broader sectoral scope, additional registration fields, specific national CSIRT reporting formats). Even where the core obligations (Article 21 risk measures, Article 23 incident reporting, governance/Article 20 duties) are broadly similar, you should expect:

- Different competent authorities and reporting portals per country (Ireland's NCSC, Spain's INCIBE/CCN, France's ANSSI).
- Possibly different registration deadlines and thresholds tied to each country's national entry-into-force date, not the EU-wide 17 October 2024 date.
- Divergent enforcement postures and fine levels within the NIS2 caps (up to €10M/2% turnover for essential entities, €7M/1.4% for important entities), since national law sets exact figures.
- For a group entity, "one incident, three jurisdictions" scenarios where you may need to notify three different CSIRTs on three different clocks if the incident affects services in all three countries.
