As of late 2026, Vietnam's personal data protection penalty regime is fully operative, and organisations face a layered exposure that combines the substantive obligations of Law No. 91/2025/QH15 (effective 1 January 2026) with the sanctions framework introduced by Decree No. 330/2026/ND-CP, which was issued and took effect on 19 August 2026 (82 articles, 33 of which deal specifically with personal data protection). This decree is the operative penalty instrument now — it implements the penalty mandate set out in Article 8 of Law 91/2025 and works alongside Decree 356/2025/ND-CP, which governs the underlying PDPIA and cross-border transfer impact assessment (CPDTIA) dossiers, the 60-day submission window to the Ministry of Public Security (A05), and the six-monthly update cycle.

The penalty tiers under Decree 330/2026 are calibrated to the severity and scale of the violation:

1. Serious cross-border violations — failure to file, concealment, or misdeclaration of a cross-border data transfer impact assessment where it results in a breach or loss of Vietnamese citizens' data — carry the heaviest exposure: a percentage of the organisation's prior-year Vietnam-market revenue. This is 1–2% for incidents affecting 10,000 to under 100,000 data subjects, 2–3% for 100,000 to under 1 million, and 3–5% for one million or more data subjects, or where there is national defence/security impact. Where the organisation has no Vietnam revenue, or the percentage calculation falls below a fixed floor, the decree substitutes fixed VND amounts: 200–500 million, 500 million–1 billion, and 1–3 billion respectively across the same tiers.

2. Unlawful data trading (Article 53) is penalized at 2–10 times the unlawful proceeds. Where proceeds cannot be determined, fixed fines of VND 500 million–1 billion apply where basic data of at least 10,000 people or sensitive data of at least 2,000 people is involved, rising to VND 1–3 billion where the conduct seriously affects national security, public order, the macroeconomy, or life, health, or dignity.

3. Administrative and procedural violations are penalized more modestly but are still material: plain non-filing or late updating of a CPDTIA dossier draws VND 30–50 million; consent violations (e.g., defective consent mechanisms) draw VND 30–50 million, rising to VND 50–70 million where silence is treated as consent or processing continues after consent withdrawal; PDPIA falsification or non-cooperation with the authority draws VND 50–100 million; and DPO appointment or qualification failures draw VND 10–30 million.

For all of these, individual violators are penalized at half the organisational rate.

Beyond fines, Decree 330/2026 empowers the Ministry of Public Security to impose remedial measures: suspension of processing activities or cross-border transfers, compulsory destruction of unlawfully processed data, disgorgement of unlawful proceeds, and confiscation of instrumentalities used in the violation. These remedial powers can be more operationally damaging than the fines themselves, particularly a suspension order against an ongoing cross-border transfer that a business depends on.

One important transitional nuance: conduct that occurred before 19 August 2026 is judged under whichever law was in force at the time, unless Decree 330/2026 happens to be more lenient — a standard "lex mitior" style transitional rule that benefits the violator only when the new regime is more favorable.

Practically, this means your compliance stack has three layers to manage simultaneously: Law 91/2025 for substantive obligations (consent, rights, security), Decree 356/2025 for the procedural PDPIA/CPDTIA mechanics and 60-day A05 filing, and Decree 330/2026 for what happens if you get any of that wrong. I'd recommend prioritizing your cross-border transfer filings and consent mechanism review first, since those carry the highest percentage-of-revenue exposure under the new decree. This is general compliance information, not legal advice — for a specific enforcement exposure assessment, consult Vietnamese counsel or an accredited assessor.
