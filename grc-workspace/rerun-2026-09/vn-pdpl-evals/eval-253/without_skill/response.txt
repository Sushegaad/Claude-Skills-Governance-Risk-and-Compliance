Vietnam's data protection framework has developed in layers rather than as a single unified code, and it's important to understand it as a stack of overlapping instruments rather than one statute (unlike GDPR, which is a single regulation).

Timeline:

- 2013: Constitution and Civil Code establish general rights to privacy and protection of personal information/honor, providing the constitutional backdrop but no operational detail.
- 2015 Civil Code (effective 2017): Article 38 codifies the right to privacy, personal and family secrets, requiring consent for collection, storage, use, and disclosure of personal information, but with no enforcement mechanism of its own.
- 2018 Law on Cybersecurity (effective 2019): Introduces broad data localization and content-control obligations for domestic and foreign service providers, with Decree 53/2022 later detailing which service providers must store certain data in Vietnam and, in some cases, establish a local presence.
- 2019–2021: Sector-specific rules accumulate — the Law on Network Information Security, e-commerce decrees (Decree 52/2013, amended by Decree 85/2021), consumer protection law provisions on personal data, and banking-sector circulars from the State Bank of Vietnam.
- Decree 13/2023/ND-CP on Personal Data Protection (effective July 1, 2023): This was the first comprehensive, GDPR-influenced instrument. It introduced defined categories of "personal data" and "sensitive personal data," a consent-centric processing model, data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure/withdrawal, restriction), controller/processor distinctions, mandatory Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) dossiers, cross-border Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) requirements, breach notification within 72 hours, and appointment of data protection personnel/departments for certain processors. It was issued as a decree (secondary legislation under existing laws), not a standalone law, which limited its penalty ceiling and created some ambiguity about supremacy versus other sector laws.
- 2023–2025: Ministry of Public Security drafts a full Personal Data Protection Law (Luật Bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân) to elevate the decree into primary legislation, closing gaps (clearer definitions, stronger penalty authority including revenue-based fines, clearer DPO/representative requirements, and alignment with international transfer regimes). Public consultation drafts circulated through 2024–2025.
- Passage and effective date (~2025–2026): The National Assembly passed the PDPL, with an effective date generally reported as taking hold in 2026 (some transition/grace periods were expected for existing businesses to align consent records, DPIA/TIA dossiers, and appointment of data protection officers).
- Ongoing (2026 and after): Implementing decrees/circulars to operationalize the PDPL are expected, similar to how Decree 13 was itself followed by guidance documents; enforcement priorities have focused on cross-border transfers, data broker/data trading crackdowns, and large platforms (e-commerce, fintech, adtech).

Structural map of the current framework:
1. Constitutional/civil law layer — foundational privacy right (Constitution 2013, Civil Code 2015).
2. Cybersecurity/localization layer — Law on Cybersecurity 2018 + Decree 53/2022, governing data localization, content, and critical information infrastructure.
3. Personal Data Protection layer — Decree 13/2023 as the operating rulebook, now superseded/elevated by the PDPL as primary law, covering consent, data subject rights, DPIA, TIA, breach notice, sensitive data categories, and enforcement.
4. Sector-specific overlays — banking/SBV circulars, telecom (Ministry of Information and Communications), e-commerce decrees, healthcare data rules, and consumer protection law provisions that add specific obligations on top of the general PDPL baseline.
5. Criminal law backstop — Penal Code provisions (notably Article 288 and related articles) criminalizing illegal trading/disclosure of personal data.
6. Enforcement bodies — primarily the Ministry of Public Security's cybersecurity and data protection department (successor to/aligned with A05/A06), with sector regulators (SBV, MIC) enforcing their own overlays.

Bottom line: Decree 13/2023 remains the operational backbone most companies built their compliance programs around, and the PDPL (effective 2026) is best understood as that decree's rules being elevated, tightened, and given sharper penalties — not a wholesale re-architecture. Companies already compliant with Decree 13's DPIA/TIA/consent/breach-notification requirements should be close to PDPL-ready, but should re-check appointment requirements for data protection officers/representatives and the new fine structure.
