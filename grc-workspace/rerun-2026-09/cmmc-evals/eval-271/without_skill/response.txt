Short answer: don't cancel it yet, but don't treat December 2026 as immovable either. Let's think through this carefully because a six-figure C3PAO engagement is a real budget decision, and the CMMC landscape has been in flux long enough that "wait and see" has genuine costs too.

First, separate two questions: (1) are you contractually or regulatorily required to have a Level 2 certification assessment by a specific date, and (2) is the C3PAO market/rule stable enough that December 2026 is still the right timing.

On the first question: CMMC 2.0 requirements are being rolled into DoD contracts via the 32 CFR CMMC program rule and the corresponding 48 CFR DFARS acquisition rule. Once a contract or solicitation includes the CMMC clause, self-assessment (Level 1 or Level 2 depending on data sensitivity) or third-party certification (Level 2 for CUI-handling contractors, Level 3 for the highest-sensitivity work) becomes a condition of award or of exercising an option period. If you already have a contract, task order, or upcoming recompete that names a CMMC Level 2 certification requirement with a real deadline, cancelling a scheduled assessment risks being unable to bid, unable to receive award, or unable to keep the contract active. That's the scenario where you should not cancel regardless of cost — the assessment cost is trivial compared to losing the contract or being found non-compliant via a False Claims Act theory (contractors have already faced FCA suits for misrepresenting their NIST 800-171 compliance posture).

If you don't yet have a hard contractual trigger — for example, you're preparing proactively, or your relevant contracts haven't yet had the clause flowed down — then there's more room to negotiate timing. In that case, ask your C3PAO:
- What's the cancellation or rescheduling fee, and how far out can you move the date without losing your deposit?
- Are they still committed to December, or are they already seeing schedule pressure/backlog that suggests you'd lose your slot anyway if you delay?

Second, look at your own readiness, not just the external timeline. A C3PAO assessment on an environment with open POA&M items beyond what's allowed, unremediated SPRS score gaps, or unfinished System Security Plan documentation is money likely spent on a failed or conditional assessment — which costs you the fee again for remediation and reassessment. If your team isn't at a mature "assessment-ready" state, it may be more valuable to push the date by a quarter and spend the interim tightening controls than to keep the December date and risk a bad outcome.

Third, factor in that CMMC has had multiple rulemaking delays and version changes since 2020 (the original 2020 interim rule, the 2021 CMMC 2.0 announcement, the 2024 proposed rule, and later phased rollout guidance). This history means "requirements might change again" is a legitimate consideration, but it has not, in past cycles, justified indefinite delay for contractors with real deadlines — those who waited generally ended up scrambling later.

My recommendation: don't cancel outright. Instead, (a) confirm your actual contractual deadline exposure with your contracts/legal team, (b) run an internal readiness check (updated SSP, current SPRS score, POA&M status) in the next few weeks, and (c) based on that, either keep December, or negotiate a shorter delay with the same C3PAO rather than cancelling and re-procuring later, since re-entering the C3PAO queue can mean a much longer wait than a simple reschedule. Cancelling outright only makes sense if you have zero near-term contractual exposure and genuine reason to believe the requirements will materially change before you'd need certification again — and even then, a reschedule preserves your spot better than a cancellation.

I'd want more specifics — your current contract portfolio's CMMC clause status and your internal audit/readiness results — before giving a firmer go/no-go.
