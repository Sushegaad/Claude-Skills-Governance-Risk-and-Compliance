This is a fair question to be tracking closely, because the answer materially affects how you should be sequencing your CMMC compliance spend right now. Here's what I'd tell a client based on general knowledge of how this has been discussed:

The phrase "CMMC Reform Task Force" (or similar reform/streamlining efforts under various names) reflects an ongoing pattern in the CMMC program's history: since the DoD first proposed CMMC in 2020, the program has gone through repeated cycles of industry pushback, internal DoD review, and revision. CMMC 1.0 (five maturity levels, mandatory third-party assessment for nearly everyone) was scrapped in 2021 in favor of CMMC 2.0, which cut the levels to three, expanded the pool of contractors eligible for self-assessment, and added a phased rollout with POA&M allowances. That 2.0 framework was finalized via the 32 CFR rule in 2024 and the DFARS acquisition rule that followed.

Any subsequent "reform" or "task force" effort you're hearing about is most likely one of a few things, and it's worth confirming which:
1. A DoD-internal or interagency working group looking at implementation friction — things like C3PAO capacity/bottlenecks, cost burden on small businesses, reciprocity with other frameworks (FedRAMP, StateRAMP, ISO 27001), or alignment with the broader NIST SP 800-171 Rev 3 update.
2. Congressional or industry-association (NDIA, PSC, AIA) pressure pushing for changes to timelines, small-business relief, or scope clarifications, which sometimes gets characterized informally as a "reform" effort even without a formally named task force.
3. A specific named initiative under the current administration's broader deregulatory or acquisition-reform push, which could touch CMMC as one of many DoD compliance requirements under review.

What's typically "actually being considered" in these efforts tends to cluster around a consistent set of themes because they're the recurring pain points contractors and C3PAOs have raised for years: (a) small and mid-size business cost relief — extended POA&M timelines, phased-in enforcement, or subsidized assessment costs; (b) C3PAO capacity constraints — there have historically been far more contractors needing Level 2 certification than accredited assessors available, causing backlogs; (c) reciprocity and control mapping — reducing duplicate audit burden for contractors who already hold FedRAMP Moderate/High or similar certifications; (d) scope and CUI-flow-down clarity — how CMMC applies to cloud service providers, subcontractors, and external service providers; and (e) alignment with NIST SP 800-171 Rev 3, which changed some control numbering and content after CMMC 2.0 was built around Rev 2.

On timing: DoD rulemaking of this kind moves slowly and unpredictably. Historically, moves from "under discussion" to "final rule" have taken 12-24+ months, with intermediate steps of proposed rule publication, a public comment period (typically 60 days), review of comments, and then a final rule. If reform recommendations require rule changes (rather than just internal guidance/memo updates), expect a similar multi-step, multi-month-to-multi-year process, not a quick pivot.

Practical guidance: don't let "reform is coming" become a reason to pause your compliance program. In every past reform cycle, the contractors who kept building toward the current requirement were better positioned than those who waited, because reforms have generally added flexibility or relief rather than eliminating the underlying NIST 800-171 control requirements — the technical bar has stayed fairly consistent even as the assessment mechanism has changed. My recommendation is to keep executing against the current 32 CFR/48 CFR CMMC 2.0 rule as the baseline, monitor the Federal Register and DoD CIO's CMMC program office announcements for anything moving from "considered" to "proposed rule," and build some schedule flexibility into any assessment contract rather than assuming the rules will hold perfectly static — but that's a hedge, not a reason to stop.
