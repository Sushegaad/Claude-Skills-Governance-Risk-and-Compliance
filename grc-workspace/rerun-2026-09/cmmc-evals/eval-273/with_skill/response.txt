Here's the mechanics of the SPRS calculation, then how to apply it to your 12 open practices.

The methodology: The SPRS score uses the DoD Assessment Methodology for NIST SP 800-171. You start at 110 points — the theoretical maximum if all 110 practices are MET. Every practice found NOT MET deducts its assigned point value from that starting 110. The score range runs from +110 (nothing open) down to −203 (nothing implemented at all). There is no partial credit: a practice that is 80% implemented gets the exact same deduction as one that's 0% implemented. It's binary — MET or NOT MET — for scoring purposes, even though your gap assessment internally may track a "partial" status for remediation planning.

Point weights aren't uniform. Each of the 110 practices is pre-assigned a weight of 5, 3, or 1 points based on its security impact, per the DoD Assessment Methodology (v2.2, Nov 2020). The highest-impact practices — the ones that cost you the most if open — are:
- AC.L2-3.1.3 (CUI flow control) — 5 pts
- IA.L2-3.5.3 (multifactor authentication) — 5 pts
- SC.L2-3.13.8 (encryption in transit / FIPS) — 5 pts
- SC.L2-3.13.11 (FIPS-validated cryptography) — 5 pts
- SI.L2-3.14.6 (monitoring for attacks) — 5 pts
- AC.L2-3.1.5 (least privilege), AU.L2-3.3.1 (audit logging), CM.L2-3.4.1 (baseline configuration) — 3 pts each

Most other practices fall into the 1- or 3-point tier. To get your actual score, you need the exact per-practice weight from the DoD Assessment Methodology document — I can't assign precise weights to your 12 without knowing which specific practices they are.

Walkthrough with your 12 open practices: Pull your gap assessment list and tag each of the 12 with its assigned weight (5, 3, or 1). Then:

Score = 110 − (sum of weights for all NOT MET practices)

Example, to illustrate the mechanics (substitute your actual 12): if your 12 open items break down as 2 five-point practices, 4 three-point practices, and 6 one-point practices, the deduction is (2×5) + (4×3) + (6×1) = 10 + 12 + 6 = 28. Score = 110 − 28 = 82.

That 82 matters beyond just being "your number." It falls below the 88-point threshold (0.8 × 110) that governs conditional certification eligibility for a C3PAO path, and it's the same score that gets posted to SPRS and reviewed by DoD contracting officers for risk-based sourcing decisions on your self-assessment path. If any of your 12 open items are on the critical-practice list above (or IR.L2-3.6.1, incident response capability), flag those first regardless of point value — those practices can never sit on a POA&M or remain open at certification; they must be fully remediated.

Practical next steps:
1. Map each of your 12 open practices to its exact point weight from the DoD Assessment Methodology.
2. Calculate your current score using the formula above.
3. Prioritize remediation of the 5-point and critical-list items first — they move your score the most and, for critical items, are non-negotiable for certification regardless of score.
4. Recalculate after each closure and update your POA&M and SSP accordingly.
5. Submit the current score to SPRS (sprs.csd.disa.mil) and have a senior company official affirm its accuracy — this affirmation is required annually even outside a full reassessment cycle, and a false affirmation carries False Claims Act exposure.
