This comes down to two things: which specific practices are eligible for POA&M treatment at all, and how long you're allowed to carry them open. Let me walk through both.

Which gaps can go on a POA&M:
Under the CMMC 2.0 Level 2 framework (self-assessment or third-party), not every unmet NIST SP 800-171 requirement is eligible for POA&M treatment. DoD deliberately limited POA&M eligibility to prevent contractors from deferring the most security-critical controls indefinitely. The general rule set is:

1. A minimum SPRS score threshold must be met before any POA&M is allowed at all. Historically, DoD guidance has required a minimum score (commonly cited as 88 out of 110) to qualify for a POA&M-based conditional certification/self-assessment — meaning you can't have so many gaps that your baseline security posture is fundamentally deficient. If your score is below that floor, you generally need to close gaps first before you're eligible to use a POA&M for the remainder.

2. Certain high-weight/highest-criticality requirements are explicitly excluded from POA&M eligibility — they must be fully implemented before you can achieve even a conditional certification. These are typically the 5-point (and some 3-point) requirements DoD has identified as foundational, such as multifactor authentication requirements and certain access control/boundary protection basics. The specific excluded list is published in DoD's assessment methodology and CMMC scoping/POA&M guidance — you should check the current published list rather than assume, because DoD has been explicit that some controls are "not POA&M-able" regardless of your overall score.

3. Any requirement not on the excluded list, and consistent with staying above the minimum score threshold, can generally be documented with a POA&M — meaning you attest to a planned remediation with a specific completion date, supported by a remediation plan, resourcing, and milestones in your SSP/POA&M documentation.

How long you can carry them:
CMMC guidance has generally set a maximum POA&M closure window of 180 days from the date of the conditional certification/assessment. This is not indefinite — you need a documented plan with a realistic date to fully close each open item within that window, and conditional certification status converts to a non-conditional (final) status only once all POA&M items are closed and validated (for self-assessments, typically via an affirmation in SPRS; for C3PAO-assessed Level 2, via a closeout review). If you don't close the items within 180 days, your conditional status expires and you can lose the certification/self-assessment validity, which has real contractual consequences if CMMC compliance is tied to contract eligibility.

Practical guidance for your 12 gaps:
- First, calculate your SPRS score with all 12 gaps open (see the standard 110-point deduction methodology) and confirm you're above the minimum POA&M-eligible threshold (commonly 88). If you're below it, prioritize closing enough gaps — particularly high-weight ones — to cross that floor before you can rely on POA&M for the remainder.
- Second, cross-check each of your 12 open practices against the current published list of POA&M-ineligible requirements (the "must be fully implemented, no POA&M allowed" list, generally covering MFA and similarly foundational controls). Any of your 12 that fall in this category cannot go on a POA&M — they need to be remediated before you self-assess/certify at all.
- Third, for the remaining eligible gaps, build POA&M entries with realistic remediation dates inside the 180-day window, resourced and tracked, since assessors and DoD reviewers will expect to see credible, specific plans rather than vague "will fix eventually" language.
- Fourth, document everything in your SSP and POA&M artifacts consistent with NIST SP 800-171A assessment objectives, since a POA&M with vague or unsupported milestones is a common reason self-assessments get challenged later.

One more consideration: because CMMC rulemaking has evolved (32 CFR/48 CFR final rules, plus the phased implementation schedule), the specific numeric thresholds (the 88-point minimum, the 180-day window) and the excluded-practice list are the kind of details that get refined in guidance updates. I'd recommend confirming the current, exact numbers against DoD's most recent published CMMC POA&M guidance before finalizing your plan, since basing a 180-day remediation commitment on outdated guidance could leave you non-compliant even after doing the work.
