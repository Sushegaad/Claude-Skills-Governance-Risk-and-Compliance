Here is a structured approach for building your CSF 2.0 Organizational Profile as a mid-size manufacturer. Manufacturing has a distinctive risk profile — IT/OT convergence, industrial control systems (ICS/SCADA) on plant floors, third-party equipment vendors with remote access, and supply chain dependencies that are often deeper and less visible than in pure IT environments — so the profile approach below is tailored accordingly, not generic.

**Step 1: Establish Business Context**

Before rating any subcategory, document:
- **Industry/mission**: production lines, product types, safety-critical processes (if any), regulatory exposure (e.g., CMMC/DFARS if you're a defense supplier, customer contractual security requirements, state privacy law if you handle employee/customer PII).
- **Legal/regulatory/contractual obligations**: customer security addenda are often the biggest driver of "must-have" target states in manufacturing — map these explicitly to GV.OC-03.
- **Key assets and Crown Jewels**: this should explicitly include OT/ICS assets (PLCs, HMIs, SCADA historians, robotics controllers) alongside traditional IT — these often carry safety and production-continuity risk that IT-only asset lists miss.
- **Risk tolerance statement**: for manufacturers, this typically centers on production uptime and safety, not just data confidentiality — your risk appetite framing (GV.RM-02) should say so explicitly, since it will drive different priorities than a typical office-based enterprise.

**Step 2: Build the Current Profile**

Assess your actual state today across all six functions using this rating scale: Not Implemented / Partially Implemented / Largely Implemented / Fully Implemented. For a mid-size manufacturer, pay particular attention to these commonly-weak areas during current-state assessment:
- ID.AM-01/02 — do you have a complete asset inventory that includes OT/plant-floor systems, not just corporate IT?
- GV.SC-04/07 — are equipment vendors and their remote-access arrangements tracked and risk-assessed?
- PR.AA-06 — physical access control at plant facilities (often stronger than logical access control in manufacturing, worth confirming rather than assuming).
- PR.PS-01/02 — configuration and patch management for OT systems, which often lag IT patch cycles due to uptime requirements.
- DE.CM-01/09 — monitoring coverage on OT networks (frequently a blind spot — many manufacturers only monitor corporate IT).

**Step 3: Build the Target Profile**

Define target states informed by:
- Customer contractual security requirements (often the fastest-moving driver — new customer contracts frequently mandate specific controls)
- Insurance/cyber-insurance underwriting requirements
- Safety and production-continuity risk tolerance
- Any applicable regulatory baseline (CMMC/NIST 800-171 if defense-adjacent, state breach-notification laws, etc.)

Highlight subcategories where regulatory/contractual obligations create a **mandatory** (not discretionary) target state — these should be flagged distinctly in your profile table since they can't be deprioritized regardless of resource constraints.

**Step 4: Profile Table Format**

| Function | Category | Subcategory | Current | Target | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------------|---------|--------|-------|
| GV | Organizational Context (GV.OC) | GV.OC-03 | Partial | Full | Customer security addenda not yet fully catalogued against controls |
| GV | Supply Chain Risk (GV.SC) | GV.SC-04 | Partial | Full | Equipment/OT vendors not yet inventoried by criticality |
| ID | Asset Management (ID.AM) | ID.AM-01 | Partial | Full | OT/plant-floor asset inventory incomplete |
| PR | Platform Security (PR.PS) | PR.PS-02 | Not Implemented | Largely Implemented | No formal OT patch/replacement cadence |
| DE | Continuous Monitoring (DE.CM) | DE.CM-01 | Not Implemented | Partial | OT network monitoring not yet deployed |
| PR | Technology Resilience (PR.IR) | PR.IR-03 | Partial | Full | Production-line failover/resilience not formally tested |

(This is illustrative — a full profile should cover all six functions with every relevant subcategory rated, not just the manufacturing hot spots above.)

**Step 5: Prioritize and Sequence**

The gap between Current and Target drives your action plan. Sequencing logic that applies well to manufacturing:
1. **Phase 1 (prerequisites)**: GV.OC and GV.RM to lock in risk tolerance framed around production/safety; ID.AM to build a *combined* IT+OT asset inventory (this is foundational — nothing else in Protect/Detect is meaningful without knowing what OT assets exist).
2. **Phase 2**: PR controls prioritized by what protects production continuity and safety first — PR.AA for both plant and office access, PR.PS for configuration/patch management adapted to OT constraints (can't always patch live production systems — document compensating controls where true patching isn't feasible).
3. **Phase 3**: DE.CM extended to OT networks (often a net-new capability, frequently requires OT-specific monitoring tooling since IT tools don't always parse ICS protocols) and DE.AE.
4. **Phase 4**: RS and RC — incident response and recovery plans that explicitly cover production-line/safety scenarios, not just IT data breach scenarios, plus the ID.IM feedback loop back into GV.
