Voluntary Self-Disclosure (VSD) under 22 CFR § 127.12 is the right move, and it is the strongest mitigating factor available to you — but timing and discipline matter from this point forward. Here is the process, step by step.

**Step 1 — Initial Notification (target: within ~30 days of discovery).**
Do not wait to have the full picture before telling DDTC something happened. Submit a brief written notification to the DDTC Office of Defense Trade Controls Compliance covering: your company name and DDTC registration number, a general description of the potential violation(s), and an estimate of the number of occurrences over the two-year period. At this stage you are not required to have root cause or final facts — you are flagging the issue and buying time to investigate properly. Request a tolling agreement in this same notification; it preserves the statute of limitations while your internal investigation proceeds, so you are not racing the clock while trying to do the investigation right.

**Step 2 — Internal Investigation (typically 30–90 days).**
Scope this broadly, not narrowly. Pull every relevant record: licenses, TAAs/MLAs and their exact scope/party lists, shipping and export documentation, emails, engineering access logs, and any prior classification decisions. Establish: what exactly was exported or disclosed (hardware, technical data, defense services), to whom (name every recipient, entity, and country), when, under what authorization (if any), and why the authorization was missing or exceeded. Put a litigation hold in place immediately so records aren't altered or lost. Identify root cause explicitly — was this a process gap, training failure, misclassification, an agreement that didn't cover the actual scope of activity, or a deliberate act? This matters because DDTC weighs willfulness heavily as an aggravating factor, so an honest, well-documented root-cause finding (even if unflattering) tends to land better than a superficial one that looks evasive later.

**Step 3 — Final VSD Report (within ~60–90 days of initial notification).**
Submit a comprehensive written report containing: a detailed factual narrative covering every violation identified (not just the ones initially estimated — update the count if the investigation surfaced more or fewer), the specific CFR sections violated for each occurrence, every party involved (internal and external, including any foreign end-users or destination countries), a full timeline, the root-cause analysis from Step 2, corrective actions already implemented, and proposed additional remediation. Two years of unauthorized exports likely means multiple distinct violation types (e.g., unlicensed hardware exports, unauthorized technical-data disclosures, possibly TAA-scope violations) — itemize each, don't aggregate them into a vague summary.

**Step 4 — DDTC Review and Resolution (often 6–18 months for cooperative VSDs).**
DDTC will review, may come back with follow-up questions, and will reach an outcome ranging from no action or a warning letter up to a civil penalty (typically reduced given VSD credit) or, in rare/egregious cases, referral for criminal review. Continued cooperation throughout this phase is itself a mitigating factor — do not treat submission of the final report as the end of your obligations.

**Mitigating factors working in your favor:** the disclosure is voluntary, presumably self-discovered rather than surfaced by a tip or audit; cooperation with DDTC through the process; and — critically — you should be building or strengthening your compliance program in parallel (Empowered Official, TCP, training, license-tracking system) so DDTC sees a program to credit, not just a promise. Prior clean history and low national-security harm also help.

**Aggravating factors to watch for and address head-on:** willfulness, senior management awareness, involvement of embargoed/proscribed destinations (22 CFR § 126.1 countries), pattern/volume across two years, and any appearance of delay or incomplete disclosure. If any of these apply to your facts, do not bury them — DDTC's own recent enforcement practice (e.g., the 2026 BAE Systems consent agreement, 104 violations over nearly six years, VSD + cooperation credit, no debarment despite unauthorized transfers to China) shows that even serious, multi-year, multi-violation cases can avoid debarment when disclosure and cooperation are genuine and the company demonstrates remediation.

**Immediate next steps for you:** (1) don't delay initial notification while you try to fully scope it — notify DDTC now with your best current estimate and request tolling; (2) engage export-control counsel before drafting the notification if you haven't already; (3) start the internal investigation and litigation hold today; (4) begin parallel remediation (fix the process gap you'll eventually have to describe) so your final report can show corrective action already underway, not just planned. This is general compliance information, not legal advice — engage qualified export-control counsel immediately given the two-year exposure window.
