Short answer: yes, intelligence-analytics software of the kind captured by USML Category XI(b) is still ITAR-controlled today, and what you heard is partially right but easy to misread — the "temporary" modification to XI(b) was extended, not allowed to lapse.

**Background on the temporary modification.** Category XI(b) covers electronic equipment "not elsewhere enumerated" that has intelligence-analysis application, and a temporary rule retained specific language — "analyze and produce information from" and "software" — within XI(b)'s scope rather than letting export-control-reform-era narrowing remove it. That temporary retention has an expiration date built into the rule, which is presumably the source of what you heard: the control is described as "temporary" because it is implemented via a sunsetting modification rather than a permanent USML text change.

**Current status.** Per the most recent regulatory action, the XI(b) temporary modification (retaining "analyze and produce information from" and "software") has been extended through August 30, 2028 (91 FR 55461). So as of today, intelligence-analytics software meeting that description remains squarely inside Category XI(b) — the sunset date moved out, it did not arrive.

**How to apply the jurisdiction test to your specific software:**
1. **USML enumeration test (22 CFR § 121.1):** Does the software perform, or is it specially designed to perform, "analyze and produce information from" functions of the kind XI(b) captures for intelligence applications? If yes, it is enumerated under the current (extended) XI(b) text.
2. **Specially designed test (22 CFR § 120.41):** Even if not a clean textual match, ask whether the software was developed, configured, or adapted to provide a critical performance parameter enabling a military/intelligence capability. If so, it likely still falls under ITAR even outside a literal XI(b) reading.
3. **EAR fallback:** If your software is a general-purpose analytics tool with no military/intelligence-specific design basis (e.g., commercial BI software that happens to be used by an intelligence customer), it may fall to the EAR/Commerce Control List instead — but "customer is an intelligence agency" does not by itself pull commercial software into XI(b); the design/functionality test is what matters.

**Practical guidance:**
- Do not treat this as decontrolled based on the "temporary" label — operationally, treat XI(b) intelligence-analytics software exactly as ITAR-controlled through at least August 30, 2028, and revisit before that date since further extension, permanent codification, or lapse are all still possible outcomes.
- If your product sits near the line (e.g., mixed commercial/intelligence use, or ambiguous "specially designed" status), this is a strong candidate for a formal Commodity Jurisdiction (CJ) determination (22 CFR § 120.4) rather than an internal judgment call — CJ requests are specifically for ambiguous enumerated-vs-not situations like this.
- Update your internal product classification records to note the extension and its new expiry date, and calendar a review well before August 30, 2028 so the classification gets re-examined rather than silently assumed to lapse or continue.
- If you export, disclose, or provide defense services involving this software to any foreign person, you still need a TAA (22 CFR § 124.1) or applicable license, and foreign-national employees with access trigger deemed-export analysis (22 CFR § 120.50) exactly as with any other Category XI item.

Bottom line: the control was extended, not sunset. Keep the software under full ITAR treatment (registration, licensing/TAA, TCP coverage for foreign nationals, record-keeping) until DDTC actually decontrols it, and build the 2028 sunset date into your compliance calendar now rather than waiting for it to become urgent. This is general compliance information, not legal advice — verify current requirements against official DDTC/Federal Register sources for your specific product and consult qualified export-control counsel for a binding classification decision.
