How the Essential Eight and the ISM relate (they're not competitors)
These two things are often confused, but they serve different purposes and neither has historically been on a path to replace the other:

- The Essential Eight is a small, prioritized set of eight mitigation strategies (application control, patch applications, configure Microsoft Office macro settings, user application hardening, restrict administrative privileges, patch operating systems, multi-factor authentication, regular backups) designed to be a practical, high-impact starting point — especially for organizations without mature security programs. It has a four-level maturity model (Maturity Level 0 through 3) that measures how rigorously each strategy is implemented.
- The ISM is the full control catalog — hundreds of controls across governance, physical security, personnel security, and the full technical stack — intended primarily for Australian government systems and increasingly referenced by industry, particularly regulated sectors and government suppliers. The Essential Eight's eight strategies are actually a subset drawn from, and cross-referenced against, ISM controls; the ISM's guidelines sections explicitly map back to Essential Eight maturity levels.

So the relationship has generally been: Essential Eight = a prioritized "top 8" on-ramp; ISM = the comprehensive framework it's drawn from. An organization maturing its security program typically starts with Essential Eight uplift and, if it's a government entity, government supplier, or otherwise needs full ISM compliance (e.g., for an IRAP assessment or PSPF requirements), expands into the full ISM control set over time. This is additive, not a replacement relationship.

What's plausible to have changed recently
Both frameworks get revised periodically:
- The Essential Eight maturity model and technical guidance get periodic refreshes (there have been updates roughly annually to reflect new threats, e.g., adjustments to MFA guidance to favor phishing-resistant methods, or updates addressing cloud environments and SaaS applications, which the original 2017-era Essential Eight wasn't designed around).
- ACSC has discussed expanding guidance for cloud-native and SaaS-heavy environments, since the original Essential Eight strategies assume more of a traditional on-premises Windows environment.
- There has been ongoing industry discussion (not confirmed as formal ACSC policy in what I can verify) about whether the Essential Eight needs a more explicit "ninth" or supplementary strategy for cloud/identity-centric environments, but I'm not treating that as fact — flagging it only as sector chatter you may have heard, which is plausibly what's prompting your question.
