A quick caveat up front: this is one of the fastest-moving areas of U.S. export control right now, and rules affecting advanced AI chips and computing capacity have been revised multiple times in a short period. I'd treat anything below as a framework for thinking about it rather than a citation you rely on without checking the current Federal Register text and BIS's published FAQs before you finalize any export decision.

Background on the "AI diffusion rule"
In its final months, the prior administration published an "Artificial Intelligence Diffusion" interim final rule that attempted to restructure how advanced AI chips (and, to a lesser extent, the closed-weight AI model access tied to them) are controlled. The core idea was a tiered country framework layered on top of the existing ECCN 3A090 (and related 4A090/3D090/3E090, etc.) controls on high-performance/advanced computing GPUs and accelerators that had already been tightened over prior years:
- A small group of close allies would face few or no new restrictions.
- A middle tier of countries would face new per-country compute/chip caps, Validated End User (VEU) style program requirements, and government-to-government agreements as an alternative path to caps.
- Countries already subject to arms embargoes or already covered by the China/Macau restrictions (and other listed destinations) would remain heavily restricted or embargoed for the most advanced chips.
The rule also introduced novel concepts like controlling "model weights" for the most advanced closed-weight AI models above certain compute thresholds, and imposing security/reporting conditions on companies wanting to use the expanded caps.

What changed after publication
This rule drew significant pushback from industry (concerns about the complexity of the tiering, the compute-cap approach, and the model-weights control) and from some U.S. trading partners who objected to being placed in the restricted middle tier. My knowledge of the exact final disposition may be out of date by the time you read this — the rule's effective date was pushed out at least once, and there was public discussion of it being replaced or substantially rewritten rather than implemented as originally drafted. Given how contested and fluid this has been, I would not rely on my recollection of the current status; you should pull the live BIS rule text (and any subsequent amendments or rescissions) before making a compliance decision.

What is much more stable, and what you should structure around regardless of how the diffusion-specific tiering ultimately lands
- The underlying ECCN classifications for advanced GPUs/accelerators (3A090.a for the most advanced parts, 3A090.b for a middle band, plus associated software/technology entries) are the backbone of chip controls and are unlikely to disappear even if the diffusion rule's country-tier overlay changes.
- The China/Macau-specific controls (including the semiconductor manufacturing equipment and advanced computing rules dating back to October 2022 and subsequent amendments) remain a hard floor — advanced AI chips above certain performance thresholds destined for China, Macau, and D:5 arms-embargoed countries have been restricted regardless of what happens to the newer diffusion tiering.
- The Entity List has been used aggressively in this space — many Chinese AI, chip-design, and cloud-computing entities have been added, and "affiliates" rules (the 50% ownership rule) mean you need to trace corporate ownership, not just check the named entity.
- Temporary General License and country-specific carve-outs have shifted repeatedly, so any compliance program in this space needs a process for re-checking classification and licensing status frequently (e.g., quarterly, or before every shipment) rather than relying on a static determination.

Practical recommendation for structuring exports
1. Classify your specific chip/accelerator SKUs against the current 3A090 performance thresholds (and check 4A090 if it's an end system, not just the chip) — don't assume a "diffusion rule" label change alters the base classification.
2. Build your country/customer matrix around the more durable China/Macau/D:5 restrictions and Entity List screening first, then layer in whatever the current diffusion-style tiering (or its replacement) requires for other destinations.
3. Because this rule has been amended, delayed, and reportedly reworked, get current legal/regulatory confirmation of its status — including whether it has been rescinded, replaced, or is in effect as originally published — before designing your export structure around it, rather than relying on an assistant's general knowledge of a rule this volatile.
