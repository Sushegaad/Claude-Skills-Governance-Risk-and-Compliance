Short answer: no — not on its own, and not anymore. This is one of the most consequential scoping traps in PCI DSS v4.0.1 right now, and it applies directly to your situation.

As a Level 1 merchant, your organisation is required to complete a full Report on Compliance (ROC) performed by a QSA — you are not eligible for any Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ), including SAQ A. SAQ A's eligibility criteria (cards not present, all cardholder data functions fully outsourced to a PCI-compliant third party, no CHD touching your systems) exist to define a reduced SAQ control set for smaller merchants. They were never designed as a scoping methodology for a ROC, and PCI SSC has now made that explicit.

FAQ 1331 (updated August 31, 2026) states that SAQ eligibility criteria may no longer be used as a guide for determining PCI DSS requirement applicability in a ROC assessment "unless explicitly reviewed, discussed and agreed upon with the merchant's compliance accepting entity" — meaning your acquiring bank and/or the relevant card brand(s), not just your QSA. This is a change from the informal practice many QSAs previously followed, where a QSA's professional judgment that "this merchant looks like an SAQ A profile" was treated as sufficient justification to mark large blocks of ROC requirements as Not Applicable (particularly around Req 6.4.3 and 11.6.1, the payment-page script inventory and tamper-detection controls that are frequently the reason merchants want to lean on SAQ-A-style scoping in the first place).

What this means concretely for you:

1. QSA agreement alone is no longer sufficient evidence. Even if your QSA is comfortable with the SAQ A logic and signs off, that agreement does not, by itself, satisfy the current PCI SSC position. You need it "explicitly reviewed, discussed and agreed upon" with your acquirer and/or card brand.
2. Get it in writing. Before your QSA finalizes the ROC with requirements marked N/A on this basis, obtain documented approval from your acquiring bank (and any card brand involved, e.g., via your acquirer) confirming they accept the SAQ-A-style scoping rationale for your specific ROC. Keep this correspondence as part of your assessment evidence file — a QSA's ROC workpapers should reference it directly.
3. Pay particular attention to 6.4.3 and 11.6.1. These e-commerce/script-integrity requirements are the ones most often incorrectly excluded via SAQ-A logic. Given that you operate a Level 1 e-commerce environment, your QSA should independently verify whether your actual payment flow (not just the SAQ-A "ideal" fully-outsourced model) truly meets the SAQ A profile — most Level 1 merchants have some degree of customer-facing payment page control, third-party script embedding, or redirect logic that would place them closer to an SAQ A-EP profile, which does carry these requirements.
4. Re-scope from first principles for the ROC. Rather than starting from "which requirements does SAQ A exclude," your QSA should build ROC applicability from the actual CDE boundary: what stores, processes, or transmits PAN/SAD, and what is connected to those systems without adequate segmentation. This is the scoping approach ROC assessments are meant to use regardless of what an equivalent SAQ profile would exclude.
5. Document the decision either way. If, after acquirer/brand review, some requirements remain justifiably N/A, document the rationale, the approving parties, and the date of agreement in the ROC. If the accepting entity does not confirm agreement, treat the requirements as in-scope and assess them fully.

Recommended next step: raise this with your QSA and acquirer now, before ROC finalization — not after submission. If the acquirer/brand agreement isn't obtained, expect your QSA to walk back any N/A markings based purely on SAQ A criteria and assess the full requirement set for your actual CDE. This is informational guidance, not a substitute for your QSA's formal determination; verify current requirements against the official PCI SSC FAQ library and your acquirer's specific expectations.
