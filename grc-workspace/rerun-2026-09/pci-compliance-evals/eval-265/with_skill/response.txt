Both mechanisms exist to handle the same problem — you can't meet a PCI DSS requirement exactly as written — but they work in fundamentally different ways, apply to different situations, and carry different documentation and assessment burdens. Here's the distinction and how to decide which one fits your case.

Compensating Control (Defined Approach only)

A compensating control is used when you're following the Defined Approach (the traditional, prescriptive PCI DSS path) but a specific technical or documented business constraint prevents you from implementing a requirement exactly as stated. Instead of an alternative way to meet the objective, a compensating control is an additional set of controls that offsets the risk of not meeting the literal requirement.

To be valid, a compensating control must:
1. Meet the intent and rigor of the original requirement.
2. Go above and beyond other PCI DSS requirements (it can't just be a control you'd need to have anyway).
3. Be commensurate with the additional risk created by not meeting the requirement as written.
4. Be documented in the ROC/SAQ using a Compensating Control Worksheet (CCW), reassessed at every annual assessment.

Typical use case: A legacy application can't support the 12-character minimum password length. As a compensating control, you might implement a shorter password combined with mandatory MFA, tighter account lockout thresholds, and enhanced logging/monitoring on that specific system — controls that collectively offset the weaker password policy.

Customised Approach (New in v4.0)

The Customised Approach is a formal alternative validation path introduced in PCI DSS v4.0. Rather than reacting to a constraint with an add-on control, you design your own control from the ground up that achieves the stated Objective of the requirement — the Objective is published by PCI SSC for each applicable requirement. This is meant for mature organizations with innovative or non-traditional architectures (cloud-native, zero-trust, microservices) where the prescriptive Defined Approach control doesn't map cleanly onto your technology.

To use the Customised Approach, you must:
1. Perform and document a Targeted Risk Analysis (TRA) for that specific control — including risk description, how the objective is achieved, and evidence of effectiveness.
2. Obtain senior management approval and sign-off on the TRA.
3. Have your QSA assess the control using a Customised Approach Test Plan (CATP) they develop specifically for your implementation (rather than applying PCI SSC's standard testing procedure).
4. Review and revalidate the control at least annually.

Typical use case: Instead of implementing a specific password-based authentication scheme, an organization with a mature zero-trust architecture might use continuous device-and-identity risk scoring plus short-lived cryptographic certificates to achieve the same authentication-assurance objective — an approach that doesn't map to any single Defined Approach control but still satisfies the underlying security intent.

Key differences at a glance:

- Available under: Compensating Control -> Defined Approach only. Customised Approach -> Customised Approach only.
- Trigger: Compensating Control -> Can't meet requirement due to technical/business constraint. Customised Approach -> Choose to meet the requirement's Objective differently.
- Nature: Compensating Control -> Additional controls that offset risk. Customised Approach -> Alternative control replacing the prescriptive one.
- Documentation: Compensating Control -> Compensating Control Worksheet (CCW). Customised Approach -> Targeted Risk Analysis (TRA) + Customised Approach Test Plan (CATP).
- Assessment: Compensating Control -> QSA reviews CCW annually. Customised Approach -> QSA develops and applies a bespoke test plan.
- Eligibility: Compensating Control -> Available for most requirements. Customised Approach -> Not available for SAQ A or SAQ B; generally used in ROC/QSA-assessed environments.
- Management sign-off: Compensating Control -> Not formally required (though good practice). Customised Approach -> Required — senior management must approve the TRA.

Which one fits your situation?

Ask: is the issue a hard constraint on a specific system (legacy app, vendor limitation) where you'd otherwise stay on the standard Defined Approach for everything else? That points to a compensating control — it's faster to document and doesn't require redesigning your assessment methodology.

Is the issue that your overall architecture is genuinely different from what the Defined Approach assumes, and you have (or can build) a mature, risk-based way of meeting the requirement's actual security objective, with the internal capability to run and defend a TRA? That points to the Customised Approach — but be aware it's a heavier lift: it requires QSA involvement to build a bespoke test plan, formal management approval, and annual revalidation, and it isn't available at all if you're validating via SAQ A or SAQ B.

Recommendation: Bring the specific requirement and constraint to your QSA or ISA early — they can confirm whether a CCW is sufficient or whether the requirement genuinely needs a TRA-backed Customised Approach, and can help scope the documentation burden accordingly before your assessment window closes.
