The answer depends on your role, because ISO/IEC 27701:2019 had a separate temporary files control for each.

**Controller**
- 2019: control 7.4.6, Temporary files (Annex A, cited as A.7.4.6).
- 2025: **A.1.4.7 Temporary Files** (Table A.1, domain A.1.4, privacy by design and by default).

**Processor**
- 2019: control 8.4.1, Temporary files (Annex B, cited as B.8.4.1).
- 2025: **A.2.4.2 Temporary Files** (Table A.2, domain A.2.4).

Both are one-for-one carry-overs. The substance did not change; only the ID did. The number moves because, in the 2025 tables, .1 in each domain is the objective statement and controls start at .2.

ISO 27701 supports GDPR alignment (Art. 5(1)(e) storage limitation, Art. 25 data protection by design). Certification is not a GDPR safe harbor.

**What the control requires**
- Controller (A.1.4.7): make sure temporary files containing PII (exports, caches, logs, scratch files, staging copies) are securely managed and deleted when no longer needed, within a documented period.
- Processor (A.2.4.2): the same for temporary files created while processing on a controller's behalf, deleted according to the agreed schedules.
- Evidence an auditor will look for: a retention and deletion rule that explicitly covers temp files, caches and logs; scheduled cleanup jobs or configuration; and spot-check results.
- Common pitfall: temp files, logs and caches with PII are left out of the retention and deletion policy and persist beyond task completion.

Numbering caution: the 2019 "Annex B" was the processor controls, while the 2025 Annex B is implementation guidance. Cite A.2.4.2 for the processor control, not an Annex B reference. For a formal SoA, confirm against the Annex F correspondence table in ISO 27701:2025.

This is general compliance information, not legal advice.
