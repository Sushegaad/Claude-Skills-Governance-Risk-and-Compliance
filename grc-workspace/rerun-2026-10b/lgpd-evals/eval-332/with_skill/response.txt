ANPD's institutional status and Consulta Pública 1/2026

1. Where ANPD stands now

Lei 15.352/2026, published in the DOU on 25 February 2026, converted Provisional Measure 1.317/2025 into law. It turned ANPD into an autarquia de regime especial, a full regulatory agency with functional, decision-making and financial autonomy. The name is unchanged.

Why this matters:
- Before, ANPD was created under Law 13.853/2019 as a federal body with technical autonomy, but it sat inside the federal administration structure. Its budget and independence were regular criticisms.
- Now it has an independent footing comparable to other Brazilian regulators. Expect stronger resourcing, more consistent enforcement, and more assertive rule-making.
- The same law fixed the effectiveness date of the Digital ECA (the children's online protection statute) at 17 March 2026, which brought ANPD into supervision of children's online services.

Alongside the institutional change, enforcement has picked up. ANPD fined ByteDance/TikTok R$153.7 million (DOU, 25 August 2026) over children's and adolescents' data, and opened 16 proceedings on a single day in June. Treat the era of slow, warning-first enforcement as over.

2. The consultation you keep hearing about

Consulta Pública 1/2026 runs from 9 September to 26 October 2026. It proposes to replace Resolution CD/ANPD 1/2021, the current sanctioning-procedure regulation. Key proposals:

| Proposal | What it would mean |
|---|---|
| Extending supervision to Digital ECA duties | Children's-protection obligations fall within the same sanctioning procedure |
| Covering internet application providers | Platforms and apps are explicitly in the supervised population |
| Interim measures without prior hearing | ANPD could order a stop, block, or other measure before you have been heard, which is a significant due-process change |
| Daily fines capped at R$50M in total | Formalises coercive daily fines, with a total ceiling |

3. What is and is not in force

This is the point most often misunderstood. The consultation is a proposal, not a rule. Until ANPD adopts the replacement:
- Resolution CD/ANPD 1/2021 remains the operative sanctioning regime.
- Resolution CD/ANPD 4/2023 (dosimetry) governs how fines are calculated.
- Art. 52 LGPD still sets the ceiling: up to 2% of Brazilian revenue, maximum R$50M per infraction.
- The existing process stays: preliminary investigation, a sanctioning proceeding with a 15-business-day defence period, a board decision, and an internal appeal within 10 business days.

4. Should you comment?

If you are an internet application provider, a platform with minors among your users, or a business that could be hit by an interim measure, yes. File before 26 October. Useful topics:
- Safeguards for interim measures without hearing (criteria, duration, review, proportionality)
- Clarity on how Digital ECA duties interact with Art. 14 LGPD
- How the R$50M daily-fine total interacts with the per-infraction cap in Art. 52
- Transition rules for open proceedings

Trade associations often file collectively. Check whether yours is doing so.

5. Related deadlines

- Order CD/ANPD 122/2026 (11 August) requires semiannual transparency reports from platforms with more than 1 million registered minor users. The first covered January to June 2026 and was due by 17 September. Later reports follow an 1 August / 1 February cadence.
- Resolution 19/2024: the grace period for ANPD standard contractual clauses ended 23 August 2025.

6. Next steps

1. Confirm whether you are in the consultation's scope (platform, app provider, minors).
2. Decide on a response by around 19 October to leave time for sign-off.
3. Review your incident and cooperation procedures, since faster proceedings mean shorter lead times.
4. Watch the final text for transition provisions.
