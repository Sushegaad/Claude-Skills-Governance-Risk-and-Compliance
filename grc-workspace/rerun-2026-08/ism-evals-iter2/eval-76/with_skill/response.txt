# ISM Applicability, Key Controls, and Authorisation Pathway for Your Cloud-Hosted Case Management System

## 1. Which control applicability markings apply

Your system stores **OFFICIAL: Sensitive (OS)** information. Under the ISM's stacking rule, that means you must implement:

- **NC (Non-Classified)** controls — the universal baseline that applies to every government system
- **OS (OFFICIAL: Sensitive)** controls — the additional layer specific to sensitive-but-unclassified information

You do **not** need to implement PROTECTED (P), SECRET (S), or TOP SECRET (TS) controls unless the system's classification changes (e.g., if it starts handling PROTECTED-level case data). If you're not fully confident the system will never touch PROTECTED information, it's worth formally re-confirming the classification decision and documenting the rationale in your System Security Plan (SSP).

## 2. Key controls you must implement

Because this is a **cloud-hosted** system, your answer needs to cover both the standard OS-baseline controls and the cloud-specific considerations.

### Core OS-baseline control areas
| Chapter | Control Area | What It Means for You |
|---|---|---|
| Ch. 1 — Cyber Security Roles | CISO/system owner accountability | Designate a system owner and ensure CISO oversight of the case management system |
| Ch. 3 — Procurement & Outsourcing | Cloud service provider (CSP) assessment | Assess and contractually bind your CSP to ISM-aligned controls |
| Ch. 4 — Documentation | SSP, risk register | Maintain a current SSP as your primary authorisation artefact |
| Ch. 6 — Personnel Security | Screening, training | Screen staff with access; annual security awareness training |
| Ch. 9 — Enterprise Mobility | MFA, MDM | MFA for any remote/admin access to the case management system |
| Ch. 13 — System Hardening | OS/app hardening, least privilege | Harden underlying VMs/containers; no standing admin access |
| Ch. 14 — System Management | Patching, backups, change management | Critical patches applied within SLA (see below); tested backups |
| Ch. 15 — System Monitoring | Logging | Centralised logging, minimum 18 months' retention for OS systems |
| Ch. 20 — Cryptography | Encryption | ASD-approved cryptography for data in transit and at rest |

**Patching note:** whatever hardening/configuration work you do on the compute layer, patch management is part of the same control set — critical patches within 48 hours (internet-facing) or 14 days (internal), high within 14 days, medium within 30 days — and you should be able to produce patch-status reports as evidence alongside your hardening baselines.

### Cloud Answer Checklist — must-cover items for a cloud-hosted system
1. **Leverage the CSP's existing IRAP report.** Don't commission a fresh assessment of the underlying cloud infrastructure — consume the CSP's current IRAP assessment report for the inherited control layer. Your agency only needs to assess your own configuration/workload layer (the case management application, its data, and your tenant configuration).
2. **Shared responsibility matrix.** Document, with the CSP, exactly which ISM controls the provider owns versus which your agency owns. Review this annually and keep it referenced in your SSP.
3. **Core control set specific to cloud:**
   - Tenant isolation
   - ASD-approved cryptography for data **in transit and at rest**
   - MFA for privileged and remote access
   - Event logging with agency access (don't rely solely on the CSP's internal logs — you need visibility)
   - Personnel security — clearances/screening for CSP support staff who could access your data
4. **Data sovereignty and residency.** Confirm where the case management data is stored, processed, and supported from, including any subcontractors or offshore support arrangements. Get contractual residency commitments — this matters particularly for case management data that may include personal/sensitive information about members of the public.
5. **Contractual assurance.** Ensure the contract covers incident notification obligations to your agency, evidence provision on request, and a right to audit.

## 3. Authorisation pathway before go-live

1. **System Security Plan (SSP)** — document the system boundary (including what's inherited from the CSP vs. what you own), classification (OS), security objectives (CIA ratings), and all selected/implemented controls.
2. **Security Risk Assessment** — identify threats, vulnerabilities, and residual risk specific to a cloud-hosted case management workload (e.g., data exposure, insider risk at the CSP, misconfiguration).
3. **IRAP Assessment** — not mandatory at OS level, but **strongly recommended**, and effectively expected given this is a government service. Scope your own IRAP assessment to your configuration/workload layer; rely on the CSP's existing IRAP report for the underlying platform.
4. **Plan of Action & Milestones (POA&M)** — track and remediate any findings from the risk assessment or IRAP review.
5. **Authorisation to Operate (ATO)** — your Authorising Official reviews residual risk (including risk inherited from the CSP) and signs off before go-live.
6. **Ongoing monitoring** — continuous monitoring, annual review of the shared-responsibility matrix, and re-assessment at least every 24 months or after significant change (e.g., CSP platform changes, new integrations, classification change).

## Bottom line
As an OS-classified system: implement NC + OS controls, treat the Cloud Answer Checklist as non-negotiable given the cloud hosting model, and run the six-step SSP → risk assessment → (recommended) IRAP → POA&M → ATO → monitoring pathway before go-live. Given this is a citizen-facing case management system, budget time for the CSP's shared-responsibility documentation and your own IRAP review — these are usually the long poles in the authorisation timeline.
