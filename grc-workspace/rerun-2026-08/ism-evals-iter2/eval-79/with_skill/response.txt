# Essential Eight (ML2) and Its Relationship to the ISM

## How the Essential Eight Relates to the ISM

The Essential Eight (E8) is **not a separate standard** — it's ASD's prioritised subset of the broader ISM control set: eight mitigation strategies selected as the highest-value controls for preventing, limiting, and recovering from common attack techniques. A few important clarifications before you plan your ML2 uplift:

- **E8 compliance ≠ full ISM compliance.** Achieving ML2 across the eight strategies addresses a meaningful slice of your risk, but it does not substitute for a full ISM gap analysis and SSP covering all applicable chapters for your system's classification.
- **Important for your FY target: the June 2026 ISM update and E8 maturity work are separate workstreams right now.** None of the 20 new controls added in the June 2026 ISM release carry an Essential Eight mapping. That means your ML2 uplift project and your June-2026 ISM delta/compliance work should be tracked and resourced as two distinct streams — closing your E8 gaps this financial year will not automatically close any June 2026 ISM gaps, and vice versa.
- For full government compliance, you need **both**: ISM controls (the complete applicable set) **and** your E8 maturity target.

## Mapping: Essential Eight Strategy → ISM Chapter(s)

| Essential Eight Strategy | Primary ISM Chapter(s) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Application control (allow-listing) | Ch. 13 — System Hardening | Prevents execution of unapproved software |
| Patch applications | Ch. 14 — System Management | Critical application patches within SLA |
| Configure Microsoft Office macro settings | Ch. 13 — System Hardening | Restrict/disable macro execution from the internet |
| User application hardening | Ch. 13 — System Hardening | Browser, Office, PDF reader hardening |
| Restrict administrative privileges | Ch. 13 — System Hardening, Ch. 6 — Personnel Security | Least privilege, privileged access management |
| Patch operating systems | Ch. 14 — System Management | Critical OS patches within SLA (≤14 days generally; tighter for internet-facing critical) |
| Multi-factor authentication (MFA) | Ch. 6, Ch. 9, Ch. 19 | MFA for remote access and privileged/important accounts |
| Regular backups | Ch. 14 — System Management | Tested, isolated/offline copies, 3-2-1 approach |

Practical implication: your gap analysis work for E8 ML2 can — and should — be cross-referenced directly into your ISM SSP under these chapters, since evidence for one largely satisfies evidence requirements for the other where scope overlaps (e.g., a patch management SLA report supports both your E8 "patch operating systems" maturity evidence and your Chapter 14 ISM evidence).

## What ML2 Requires for Each Strategy

ASD's Essential Eight Maturity Model defines maturity levels per strategy, not a single blanket bar. **ML2** is the ASD-recommended baseline maturity target for most government entities in 2026, and generally represents "mostly aligned — addresses moderately sophisticated adversary tradecraft," a meaningful step up from ML1 (which addresses only opportunistic, low-sophistication attacks). Directionally, ML2 for each strategy means:

- **Application control** — allow-listing extended beyond workstations to internet-facing servers, with regular rule-set review and centrally managed enforcement (not just default/audit mode).
- **Patch applications** — automated asset discovery and patching of internet-facing services within 48 hours for critical vulnerabilities/no patch available mitigations, and vulnerability scanning conducted at least fortnightly for internet-facing services.
- **Configure Microsoft Office macros** — macros from the internet blocked; only vetted, digitally signed macros from trusted locations allowed to run, with logging of macro execution attempts.
- **User application hardening** — web browsers hardened to block/prompt for Flash, ads, and Java (as applicable), and application hardening centrally managed and enforced, not just configured per-device.
- **Restrict administrative privileges** — privileged accounts explicitly identified, disallowed from accessing email/web browsing, and access to privileged accounts and their activities logged and reviewed.
- **Patch operating systems** — internet-facing services patched within 48 hours of critical vulnerability disclosure (or mitigated if no patch exists); regular vulnerability scanning; unsupported OS versions removed or isolated.
- **Multi-factor authentication** — MFA enforced for all users when authenticating to internet-facing services and for privileged actions, using verifier-impersonation-resistant methods where practical.
- **Regular backups** — backups of important data, software, and configuration settings performed and retained per business criticality, with restoration tested at least annually, and backups protected from unauthorised access/modification.

**Recommendation:** because exact ML2 wording and technical thresholds are periodically refined by ASD, validate each of the above against the current published Essential Eight Maturity Model (and the Essential Eight-to-ISM mapping document) before you finalise your FY compliance evidence — I've given you the directional shape of ML2 per strategy, but the authoritative technical thresholds should be pulled straight from ASD's current publication.

## Suggested Approach for Your FY Target
1. Run a per-strategy gap analysis against ML1 → ML2 requirements (current state vs. target for each of the eight strategies).
2. Cross-map each finding into the corresponding ISM chapter (13/14/6/9/19 as above) so the evidence feeds both your E8 maturity assessment and your ISM SSP.
3. Track the June 2026 ISM delta separately — don't assume E8 ML2 work closes any of the new AI, cryptography, or other June 2026 control additions.
4. Prioritise patch operating systems, MFA, and restrict admin privileges first if resourcing is constrained — these tend to have the highest risk-reduction-per-effort ratio and the most overlap with baseline ISM chapters.
