# CMMC Level 2 — Plain-English Guide for a First-Time DoD Contractor

Congratulations on the contract. Here's what DFARS 252.204-7021 and "CMMC Level 2" actually mean for a 45-person aerospace firm that has never dealt with this before.

## Program status you need to know right now (as of August 15, 2026)

On July 13, 2026, DoD suspended **CMMC Phase 2** — the part of the rollout that would have required third-party (C3PAO) assessments in new contracts starting November 10, 2026. This is currently in a **60-day CMMC Reform Task Force review**, with a report due around **September 13, 2026**. Two things follow from this:

- **What hasn't changed**: 32 CFR Part 170 and DFARS 252.204-7012/7019/7020/7021 are still the law. Your obligation to implement NIST SP 800-171, self-assess, submit a score to SPRS, and have your senior official affirm it annually is unaffected.
- **What has changed**: Contracting officers can currently only designate **Level 1 (Self)** or **Level 2 (Self)** — not Level 2 (C3PAO third-party) or Level 3. C3PAO/DIBCAC requirements are being stripped from solicitations and existing contracts at the next option/modification.

**Bottom line for you**: don't slow down. Build your program as if a third-party assessment could still happen — the task force could restore that requirement on short notice — but for now your near-term compliance path is very likely a **self-assessment** submitted to SPRS, not a C3PAO audit. Re-check this status after mid-September 2026, since it directly affects which assessment track your contract actually requires.

## Where CMMC sits in your contract

CMMC doesn't stand alone — DFARS 252.204-7021 is one of a family of clauses that typically travel together:

- **DFARS 252.204-7012** — requires you to safeguard CUI and report cyber incidents to the DoD's DIBNET portal within **72 hours** of discovery. This applies regardless of your CMMC level or the current suspension.
- **DFARS 252.204-7019** — requires a current NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment score on file.
- **DFARS 252.204-7020** — requires you to post that score to SPRS (Supplier Performance Risk System) and give DoD access to the underlying assessment.
- **DFARS 252.204-7021** — the CMMC certification/assessment requirement itself, and the flow-down obligation if you use subcontractors.

## What "Level 2" actually requires

Since your contract cites 7021, the required level should be stated explicitly in Section L or the Performance Work Statement — check that first rather than assuming. Level 2 means:

- You handle **CUI (Controlled Unclassified Information)** — not just FCI — somewhere in your contract performance.
- You must implement **all 110 practices** from NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2, spanning 14 security domains (access control, MFA/identification, audit logging, configuration management, incident response, encryption, media protection, physical security, personnel screening, etc.).
- You calculate a **Basic Assessment SPRS score** (110 points max, deductions of 5/3/1 per unmet practice) and submit it at sprs.csd.disa.mil, with a senior company official affirming its accuracy — annually, even between full reassessments. False affirmations carry **False Claims Act** exposure, so don't submit a score you can't back up with evidence.
- Depending on whether your program is deemed "critical," you'd either self-assess (current interim default, given the Phase 2 suspension) or eventually undergo a triennial **C3PAO** third-party assessment (paused for now).

## What you need to do, step by step

1. **Scope your CUI environment.** Identify exactly where CUI enters, is stored, processed, and transmitted — engineering drawings, technical data, and export-controlled information are the most common CUI types in aerospace contracts. Everything outside that boundary can potentially be scoped out.
2. **Build a System Security Plan (SSP).** This is the foundational document — one entry per practice, describing what's actually implemented (not aspirational). It's a prerequisite for any self-assessment or future third-party assessment.
3. **Run a gap assessment** against all 110 practices and produce a Plan of Action & Milestones (POA&M) for anything not yet met.
4. **Remediate the highest-impact gaps first** — MFA (IA.L2-3.5.3), CUI flow control (AC.L2-3.1.3), FIPS-validated encryption (SC.L2-3.13.8/3.13.11), and attack monitoring (SI.L2-3.14.6) carry the heaviest SPRS score deductions and are also practices that can never sit in an open POA&M at certification time.
5. **Calculate your SPRS score and submit it**, with senior-official affirmation.
6. **Consider FedRAMP-authorized cloud services** (Moderate or equivalent) for anything touching CUI — this is required under DFARS 7012.

## Realistic timeline

For a 45-person firm starting from zero, a commonly cited planning window is **9–18 months** from initial gap assessment to being ready for a full third-party (C3PAO) assessment — covering SSP development, technical remediation (MFA rollout, encryption, network segmentation, logging), policy documentation, and training. Given the current Phase 2 suspension, your immediate deliverable is likely the **self-assessment and SPRS submission** (which you should aim to complete on a much shorter timeline, realistically 3–6 months, since it doesn't require scheduling an external assessor) — while still building toward full readiness in case third-party assessment requirements are reinstated after the task force review.

## Practical next step

Confirm with your contracting officer or a read of Section L/PWS whether your specific program has been flagged "critical" (which would eventually require C3PAO/Level 3) or not, and whether the 7021 clause in your specific contract has already been modified in light of the July 2026 suspension. Then start the SSP and gap assessment — those don't change regardless of which assessment track ultimately applies.

*This is general compliance information, not legal advice. Verify current requirements against official DoD/CMMC-AB sources and consult qualified counsel or an accredited assessor before making certification decisions.*
