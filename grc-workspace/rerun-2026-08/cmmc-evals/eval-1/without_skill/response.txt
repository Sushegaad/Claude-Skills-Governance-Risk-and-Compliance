# CMMC Level 2: What It Means for Your Firm

Congratulations on the new DoD contract. Here's a plain-English rundown of what you're now on the hook for.

## What is CMMC?

The Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) is the Department of Defense's program for verifying that contractors who handle sensitive DoD information have adequate cybersecurity controls in place. It exists because too many contractors were self-attesting to security requirements they weren't actually meeting, and CUI (Controlled Unclassified Information) kept leaking through the supply chain.

The clause in your contract, DFARS 252.204-7021, is the mechanism that makes CMMC certification a condition of contract award and performance. If you don't have the required certification level at the time specified in the contract, you can be found non-compliant, which can affect award, exercise of option periods, or continued performance.

## What does "Level 2" mean?

CMMC has three levels:

- **Level 1 (Foundational)** — for contractors handling only Federal Contract Information (FCI). Self-assessment, 17 basic practices.
- **Level 2 (Advanced)** — for contractors handling Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI). This is you. It maps to the 110 security requirements in NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2, organized across 14 domains (Access Control, Audit & Accountability, Configuration Management, Identification & Authentication, Incident Response, Maintenance, Media Protection, Personnel Security, Physical Protection, Risk Assessment, Security Assessment, System & Communications Protection, System & Information Integrity, and Awareness & Training).
- **Level 3 (Expert)** — for contractors handling the highest-priority CUI, with additional requirements beyond NIST 800-171. This is unlikely to apply to a 45-person aerospace engineering firm doing its first DoD contract, but check your contract's specific requirement.

Since your contract cites Level 2, you almost certainly handle or generate CUI — likely engineering drawings, technical data, specifications, or other controlled technical information tied to the program you're supporting.

## Level 2 has two flavors: self-assessment vs. certification

This is the part that trips people up. Level 2 requirements can be met in one of two ways, depending on what the specific contract or DFARS clause requires:

1. **Level 2 Self-Assessment** — you assess yourself against all 110 practices and submit the score to the Supplier Performance Risk System (SPRS).
2. **Level 2 Certification Assessment** — a third-party assessment organization (a C3PAO — CMMC Third-Party Assessment Organization) formally assesses and certifies you.

Most contracts involving CUI, especially anything tied to critical programs, will require the third-party certification assessment. Check the specific contract language or ask your contracting officer which applies to you — this materially changes your timeline and cost.

## What you actually need to do

1. **Scope your environment.** Identify exactly where CUI lives, flows, and is processed, stored, or transmitted — systems, applications, cloud services, network segments, and people. A tightly scoped "CUI enclave" (isolating CUI to fewer systems) is usually far cheaper to secure and certify than trying to bring your whole company into scope.

2. **Get a copy of NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 (and be aware Rev 3 is in the pipeline for future DFARS updates)** and use it as your control checklist. Each of the 110 requirements needs to be either implemented, or documented in a Plan of Action & Milestones (POA&M) for practices not yet met (note: even under CMMC's POA&M provisions, some practices can never be POA&M'd — they must be fully met at time of assessment).

3. **Conduct a gap assessment.** Compare your current IT environment and security practices against all 110 requirements. For a 45-person firm that's "never heard of CMMC," expect meaningful gaps — this is normal, not a sign you're behind schedule as long as you start now.

4. **Build (or finish) your System Security Plan (SSP) and POA&M.** The SSP documents how each of the 110 requirements is met in your specific environment. This is a mandatory artifact regardless of assessment type.

5. **Remediate gaps.** Typical gap areas for firms at your stage: multifactor authentication (MFA) not enforced everywhere it needs to be, no FIPS-validated encryption for CUI at rest/in transit, incomplete audit logging, no formal incident response plan/testing, weak access control segmentation, missing security awareness training program, and no documented configuration management process.

6. **Score yourself in SPRS.** Before any certification event, you should have a current NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment score entered in SPRS — this is already a DFARS 252.204-7012 obligation you may already be behind on if you haven't done it.

7. **Engage a C3PAO (if certification-level is required).** Contact an accredited C3PAO from the Cyber-AB Marketplace. Many firms first hire a CMMC Registered Practitioner (RP) or RPO (Registered Practitioner Organization) to help prepare — this is optional but often worth it for a first-timer.

8. **Undergo the assessment**, remediate any findings, and receive your certification (valid for 3 years, with annual affirmations required).

## Roughly how long will this take?

For a company that's completely new to this:

- **Gap assessment + scoping:** 4-8 weeks
- **Remediation** (the biggest variable — depends heavily on your current state): 4-12 months. Technical fixes like MFA and encryption can move fast; policy/process maturity (incident response testing, audit log review processes, configuration management discipline) takes longer because assessors want to see evidence of practices being *followed*, not just documented, often over a period of months.
- **SSP/POA&M finalization:** runs in parallel with remediation, finalized near the end
- **C3PAO scheduling and assessment:** C3PAO calendars can be backed up; budget 1-3 months of lead time to schedule, plus 1-4 weeks for the assessment itself depending on scope
- **Total, realistically:** 9-18 months from a cold start to certification for a company your size with no prior NIST 800-171 program. If you already have decent IT hygiene, you could compress this; if your network is flat and CUI is scattered, expect the longer end.

## Immediate next steps this week

1. Confirm with your contracting officer/prime whether your contract requires Level 2 self-assessment or full C3PAO certification, and the deadline.
2. Identify where CUI currently lives in your environment (informal inventory).
3. Get a current NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment score into SPRS if you don't already have one — this is likely already overdue under 7012.
4. Start budgeting for both remediation costs (MFA, encryption, possibly a GCC High or similar CUI-capable cloud environment) and assessment/consulting costs.
5. Consider engaging a CMMC-knowledgeable consultant or RPO to help scope and pace the work — for a first CMMC effort, this usually pays for itself in avoided false starts.

**Bottom line:** this is a real, multi-month program of work, not a checkbox exercise — but it's very achievable for a company your size if you start scoping now rather than waiting until closer to your certification deadline.
