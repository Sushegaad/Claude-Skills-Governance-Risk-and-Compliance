# CV Screening Tool — EU AI Act Classification

## 1. AI system definition check
An AI-powered CV screening tool that ranks applicants infers, from inputs (CVs/applications), outputs (rankings/scores) that influence a real-world decision — this meets the Art. 3(1) AI system definition.

## 2. Prohibited practices screen (Art. 5)
Ranking applicants on job-relevant criteria is not one of the 9 prohibited categories (no subliminal manipulation, social scoring, or biometric categorisation involved on the facts given) — **not prohibited**, proceed to risk-tier determination.

## 3. Risk tier determination — High-Risk, Annex III Area 4(a)
**Yes, this is high-risk.**

- **Path:** Art. 6(2) / Annex III (not the Art. 6(1) sectoral-product path).
- **Annex III area: Area 4 — Employment, Workers Management, and Self-Employment, point (a) — Recruitment and selection**, specifically "filtering or screening job applications" and "evaluat[ing] candidates." A tool that *ranks* applicants for employers falls squarely within this description.
- **Art. 6(3) exceptions test:** None of the narrow exceptions (procedural task only / improving a completed human activity / pattern-deviation detection / preparatory task) plausibly apply — ranking applicants *is* the substantive evaluation, not a preparatory or narrow procedural step.
- **Profiling override (Art. 6(3), final subparagraph):** Even if an exception were argued, a CV-ranking tool profiles natural persons (evaluating work-related attributes/suitability under Art. 3(52)), which makes it high-risk regardless. There is no credible path to a non-high-risk conclusion here.
- **Conclusion:** High-risk under Annex III Area 4(a). No Art. 6(4) documentation-of-exemption route is realistically available — this system does not escape classification.

## 4. Compliance deadline
Annex III standalone high-risk systems must comply by **2 December 2027** (per the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026, which deferred the original 2 August 2026 date). As of today (15 August 2026), you have until that date to complete conformity work, but given the scope of the obligations below, work should start now.

Note also that **Art. 50 transparency obligations are already in force (2 August 2026)** — if the tool or any related interface involves an AI chatbot or AI-generated content toward candidates, that transparency duty applies now, independent of the Annex III high-risk deadline.

## 5. Main obligations as provider
As the **provider** (you develop and place the system on the market for employers), your obligations run under Arts. 9–17, 43–49:

| Requirement | Article | What it means for a CV-ranking tool |
|---|---|---|
| Risk management system | Art. 9 | Continuous 5-step process — identify risks (e.g., discriminatory ranking against protected groups), evaluate, mitigate by design first, then via information to deployers; document reasoning |
| Data governance | Art. 10 | Training/validation/test data must be representative of the applicant pools the system will encounter; document bias examination; special-category data (e.g., inferred protected characteristics) only under strict Art. 10(5) conditions |
| Technical documentation | Art. 11 | Annex IV file: development process, training data description, validation/testing metrics, risk management documentation — retained 10 years |
| Record-keeping/logging | Art. 12 | Build in automatic event logging so ranking decisions can be reconstructed post-hoc |
| Transparency to deployers | Art. 13 | Instructions for use covering accuracy/robustness metrics, known limitations, human oversight measures, input data specifications |
| Human oversight | Art. 14 | Design the system so employer staff can understand outputs, recognize automation bias, and override/disregard rankings — not a fully automated hire/reject decision |
| Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity | Art. 15 | Declared accuracy levels in instructions for use; resilience to adversarial manipulation (e.g., CV keyword-stuffing attacks) |
| Provider checklist | Art. 16 | Full 12-item list: conformity, labeling, QMS, technical file, logs, conformity assessment, EU Declaration of Conformity, CE marking, EU AI database registration, corrective action process, demonstrate conformity on request, accessible design |
| Quality management system | Art. 17 | 13-component documented QMS |
| Conformity assessment | Art. 43 | Annex III Area 4 is a **Points 2–8** area → **self-assessment only** (Annex VI-style internal control); no notified body route required |
| EU Declaration of Conformity | Art. 47 | Drawn up before market placement; retained 10 years |
| CE marking | Art. 48 | Affixed after successful self-assessment |
| EU AI database registration | Art. 49 | Register before placing the system on the market |

Separately, note that **employer-deployers** using your tool carry independent Art. 26 obligations (instructions compliance, human oversight assignment, worker notification before deployment, 6-month log retention, and — because Area 4 decisions affect natural persons — informing affected candidates under Art. 26(11)). You should factor these into your Art. 13 instructions for use so employers can meet them.
