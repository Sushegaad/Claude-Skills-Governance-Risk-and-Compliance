# Can a US healthtech host a Dubai hospital's patient data in a US cloud?

## Short answer

Not by default, and likely not without specific approvals/safeguards. Patient health data generated by a healthcare provider licensed in Dubai is typically subject to **health-data-specific localization and governance rules** from the **Dubai Health Authority (DHA)**, layered on top of the **UAE federal PDPL's cross-border transfer restrictions** for sensitive personal data. You should assume UAE-based (or DHA-approved) hosting is required unless you can affirmatively satisfy an approved transfer mechanism.

## The layers of regulation at play

1. **Dubai Health Authority (DHA) health data rules.** DHA has its own health data protection framework (issued under Dubai's health sector legislation) that has historically required health/patient data generated by DHA-licensed and regulated healthcare providers to be stored on servers physically located within the UAE, or otherwise to go through a DHA approval process before any cross-border hosting or transfer. This is the single biggest constraint for your scenario — a private hospital operating under DHA licensing is expected to keep its regulated patient records data within the UAE (or through DHA-sanctioned arrangements) rather than freely hosting them abroad.
2. **Federal PDPL — sensitive personal data.** Health data is classified as "sensitive personal data" under the UAE federal Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021), which imposes a higher bar: generally requiring explicit consent (or another narrow lawful basis) for processing, plus compliance with cross-border transfer conditions before data can leave the UAE. Cross-border transfer is only permitted where: (a) the destination country/territory has an adequate level of protection as recognized by the UAE Data Office/Cabinet, (b) appropriate safeguards are in place (e.g., contractual clauses, binding corporate rules), (c) a specific permit is obtained from the Data Office, or (d) a narrow exception applies (e.g., explicit consent plus necessity, performance of a contract, vital interest, legal claims).
3. **US adequacy status.** The US has not historically been treated as having a blanket "adequate" data protection status recognized by UAE regulators in the way, say, an EU member state might be considered under GDPR-style adequacy regimes. That means you likely cannot rely on adequacy alone — you'd need contractual safeguards (data processing agreement with UAE-compliant clauses), possibly a specific regulatory permit, and, more fundamentally, DHA clearance for the health-sector-specific localization requirement.
4. **Hospital's own obligations as data controller.** The Dubai hospital, as the licensed healthcare provider and likely data controller, bears primary regulatory responsibility for where its patient data is hosted and how it is processed. You, as the SaaS vendor, would typically act as a data processor/sub-processor, and the hospital's compliance team/DHA license conditions will drive what is actually permissible — they cannot simply outsource that judgment to you.

## What this means practically for your deal

- **Assume you need UAE-based hosting for DHA-regulated patient data**, or at minimum a documented DHA approval/exception for offshore hosting, before contracting proceeds.
- If full data residency in the US is a hard business requirement, look at options like: (a) deploying a UAE region/data center (directly or via a hyperscaler's UAE region) for the patient data workload, (b) architecting a hybrid model where identifiable patient health records stay in-UAE while de-identified/aggregated analytics data is processed in the US, or (c) securing explicit DHA/Data Office approval for the specific cross-border arrangement, backed by a robust data processing agreement, encryption, and access controls.
- Build in **explicit, specific patient consent** language as one supporting layer, but don't treat consent alone as sufficient — DHA's localization posture for regulated health records is generally treated as a stricter, more binding constraint than a "consent unlocks anything" model.
- Confirm whether the hospital sits under **DHA** (Dubai) versus **DoH** (Department of Health, Abu Dhabi) — if the engagement expands to Abu Dhabi facilities, DoH has its own, separately administered health data regulations you'd need to check as well.
- Put a clear data residency and sub-processor disclosure clause in your MSA/DPA with the hospital, and be prepared to support a joint compliance review or attestation the hospital may need to submit to DHA.

*This reflects general understanding of DHA health data governance and the UAE federal PDPL cross-border transfer regime; given the sensitivity (regulated health data, cross-border, US host), get UAE healthcare-regulatory counsel to confirm current DHA rules and any available approval pathway before finalizing architecture.*
